Gyeongbuk council presses for feasibility checks on delayed sunlight villages

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.20

In an audit on the 17th, a North Gyeongsang provincial council committee raised land and grid hurdles, 440 million won in losses from halted gas works in the north, and a revived biomass plant in Pohang.

A North Gyeongsang Provincial Council audit has heard that the province's Haetbit (sunlight) Income Village projects are being delayed by difficulties in using state and public land and securing grid capacity. The council's planning and economy committee audited the provincial Science and Industry Bureau and Energy Industry Bureau on the 17th and made its findings public on the 18th. Committee member Do Hui-jae (Seongju) asked the province to examine project feasibility, costs and returns in advance so that projects do not collapse or leave residents with lower income, and to fully inform residents of project conditions and constraints.

According to the results of the second round announced by the Ministry of the Interior and Safety on October 7, 65 villages in North Gyeongsang applied and 56 were selected. Of the 436 villages selected nationwide in the second round, 413 were conditional selections that still needed to complete items such as land lease talks, financing plans or grid connection. A total of 523 villages have been selected nationwide across the first and second rounds, and third-round results are due at the end of December.

Vice-chair Woo Chung-mu (Yeongju) said that halted work on indoor city gas pipes in northern areas including Yeongju, Andong, Yecheon, Bonghwa and Mungyeong had left many older residents with losses of more than 440 million won. Woo called for prompt relief, such as free legal aid and loans for the cost of installing gas user facilities.

Committee member Jeong Suk-gyeong (proportional) said the revived plan for a biomass power plant in Pohang should be reassessed for its economics and environmental impact in light of changes in government renewable energy policy, and asked for clear standards and principles for new biomass power projects. Committee member Yun Jong-ho (Gumi) asked that any bid to attract AI data centers take into account rising demand for power and water, the handling of waste heat and wastewater, and possible burdens on residents. Committee chair Kim Chang-hyeok (Gumi) urged the province to consider in advance the service life, replacement and disposal of solar and wind equipment, and to prepare after-care measures so facilities are not left idle for long, citing wind turbine parts supply and fire risks from ageing equipment. Committee member Choi Byeong-geun (Gimcheon) pointed out that only around 50% of radiation emergency staff in the province and some cities and counties had completed their training.

Citizen Energy View

North Gyeongsang had 56 of 65 applicant villages selected in the second round, but in the face of land and grid barriers, selection does not automatically become a power plant. If projects collapse, villages lose not only the income they expected but also some of the trust they placed in public programs. Village cooperatives should not be left to solve land, grid and financing problems alone; the province should first map the public land and grid conditions available and put them in front of villages. The province should publish, village by village, the feasibility reviews the council asked for so residents can judge for themselves.
