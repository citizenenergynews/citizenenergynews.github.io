Draft 30-year spent nuclear fuel plan open for public comment until Nov. 23, with on-site storage expected to fill up from Hanbit in 2030

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.19

Draft 3rd Basic Plan for High-Level Radioactive Waste Management (2027-2056) on public review, 552,478 assemblies so far / Regional briefings only in the Daegu, Busan and Gwangju nuclear regions; responses to commenters due by Dec. 23

Korea's High-Level Radioactive Waste Management Committee is taking public comments until Nov. 23 on the draft "3rd Basic Plan for High-Level Radioactive Waste Management", which sets the direction for managing spent nuclear fuel over the 30 years from 2027 to 2056. The draft is published in the electronic official gazette and on the committee's website, and regional briefings were scheduled in Daegu on Nov. 16, Busan on Nov. 17 and Gwangju on Nov. 18.

According to the draft, Korea's commercial reactors had produced 552,478 spent fuel assemblies (about 20,000 tonnes of uranium) by the third quarter of 2026, and on the basis of the 35 reactors in the 11th Basic Plan for Electricity Supply and Demand about 210,000 more will be produced, for a cumulative total of about 762,000. The draft expects on-site storage to fill up first at Hanbit in 2030, followed by Hanul in 2031, Kori in 2032 and Wolsong in 2037 (the timing may change with reactor operations).

The special act on high-level waste requires efforts to start operating an interim storage facility before 2050 and a disposal facility before 2060. The committee will tell commenters the results of its review by Dec. 23, then finalise the plan after deliberation and a vote by the Atomic Energy Promotion Commission and a report to the relevant National Assembly committee.

Citizen Energy View

Spent fuel is a burden left jointly by every region that used electricity from nuclear plants, yet the briefings were scheduled only in the three regions that host reactors. Residents of the capital region, the biggest electricity users, can also download the opinion form attached to the committee's notice (No. 2026-20) and submit comments by email or post until the 23rd. Since the draft itself says waste volumes have grown compared with the second plan because more reactors are operating, plans to add reactors and plans for their waste should be weighed together. Expanding renewable energy produced and used locally is the way to shrink the burden handed to the next generation.
