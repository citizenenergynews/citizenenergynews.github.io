COP31 ends without cover decision, first such failure in 17 years

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.23

The conference in Antalya, Türkiye, closed early on the 23rd, two days late. Parties split over wording on transitioning away from fossil fuels, while the just transition work programme was extended.

The 31st Conference of the Parties to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (COP31) in Antalya, Türkiye, ended at around 1 a.m. on November 23 (Korea time) without a cover decision. It closed two days later than the scheduled final day of the 20th. Korea's Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment said on the 23rd that this was the first time in 17 years, since the 2009 Copenhagen conference, that a COP had failed to reach a comprehensive agreement on its main response direction.

The draft decision prepared by COP President Türkiye and negotiations President Australia included minimising the size and duration of overshoot beyond 1.5 degrees, expanding electrification and scaling up climate finance. Parties remained divided to the end over whether to include a transition away from fossil fuels in the text. No conclusion was reached on whether to continue the mitigation work programme beyond 2027. Developed and developing countries clashed over how to link it with the Global Stocktake, and the item was pushed to the 2027 meeting of the Parties to the Paris Agreement.

Parties agreed to extend the just transition work programme (a framework in which civil society and others discuss fair transition pathways). However, the just transition mechanism, which is meant to support countries in carrying out their transitions, will be discussed again at the next session because its institutions and functions could not be settled. On adaptation, parties agreed to form a 36-member expert task force that will develop measurement methods for the 59 Belém adaptation indicators by 2027. They did not agree on the baseline and pathway for efforts to at least triple adaptation finance by 2035.

Korea's delegation was led by Minister of Climate, Energy and Environment Kim Sung-hwan, and the conference drew a record of about 100,000 participants. On November 11 the Korean government signed the Global Electrification Pledge to raise electricity's share of final energy consumption worldwide to 35% by 2035. The next conference, COP32, will be held in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, in November 2027.

Citizen Energy View

Agreement among nations broke down for the first time in 17 years, yet Korea signed a pledge at this conference to raise the share of electricity to 35% by 2035. The extra renewable power that requires does not have to wait for international talks; it can be built now on village rooftops and public land. The conference's decision to keep discussing a just transition also shows how strong the demand is for communities to share both the burdens and the benefits of the transition. The government should set a numerical target for resident- and citizen-owned renewable energy in line with the electrification pledge, and citizens should invest directly in solar power in their own neighbourhoods.
