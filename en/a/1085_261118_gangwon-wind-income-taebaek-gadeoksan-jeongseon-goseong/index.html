Gangwon Localities Spread "Wind Income": Taebaek Gadeoksan Dividends Reach 11.9 Billion Won for the Province, Goseong Eyes 20% Resident Stake

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.18

Gov. Woo Sang-ho unveils 1GW public wind plan, expects 11,667 residents to invest 10% of project costs

As Gangwon Special Self-Governing Province lays out a plan to expand wind capacity to 5.5GW within 10 years (equivalent to five nuclear reactors), local governments across the province are pursuing "wind income" (바람소득) schemes that return generation revenue to residents. The Gadeoksan (가덕산) wind farm in Taebaek has paid dividends from power sales to the province and Taebaek City, which invested in it, since 2018; with two phases completed, cumulative dividends stand at about 11.9 billion won for the province and about 7.6 billion won for the city. Residents who joined through a fund earn roughly 8.2 to 11 percent a year, and Taebaek spends its dividends on welfare such as support for infertile couples, childbirth and child-rearing aid, and study grants, and plans to increase its investment in a third phase around 2027. Pyeongchang County is set to sign a public-led onshore wind agreement with Korea East-West Power and Gangwon Development Corporation on the 18th, and Jeongseon County is also pursuing a wind income project. Goseong County will push the Heulli wind farm (seven 7.0MW-class turbines), stalled for years, as a resident-led project, with plans to set up a special purpose company (SPC) in which Goseong County would hold 30 percent and residents 20 percent. Gov. Woo Sang-ho announced a plan to introduce 1GW of "public wind" over 10 years, expects 11,667 residents to take part by investing 10 percent of project costs, and estimates annual generation sales of 473 billion won and annual public dividend income of 56 billion won once completed.

Citizen Energy View

Gadeoksan has already produced resident returns of 8.2 to 11 percent a year and dividends of about 11.9 billion won for the province and about 7.6 billion won for the city, so the point that wind profits can stay local is confirmed by numbers. Denmark required by law from 2009 that wind developers offer nearby residents the first chance to buy 20 percent of the ownership. Concern that profits leak out of villages can be answered by structures in which residents and local governments own shares directly, and Goseong's 20 percent resident stake and Gov. Woo's plan for 10 percent resident investment point in that direction. Where projects are moving ahead, as in Jeongseon, resident shareholding ratios, dividend order and the way operating information is disclosed should be written into the plan from the planning stage.
