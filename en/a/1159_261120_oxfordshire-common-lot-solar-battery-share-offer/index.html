Oxfordshire community solar and battery park seeks £500,000

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.20

Low Carbon Hub is raising funds for a 7.4MW solar park with 10MWh of battery storage until February 5, 2027. As of the 20th, 89 investors had put in £121,545.

Low Carbon Hub, a community energy society in Oxfordshire, England, is raising community investment to build Common Lot, a power plant combining 7.4MW of solar and 10MWh of battery storage. The offer opened on November 5 and runs until February 5, 2027, with a target of at least £500,000. According to the investment platform Ethex, 89 investors had put in £121,545 as of the 20th, reaching 24% of the target.

People can invest from £100 to £100,000, and the society's target interest rate is 5% a year. The money goes into the society's Community Energy Fund to help build the plant, and investors become members. The society estimates the plant will generate 7.9GWh a year, enough for more than 3,000 homes, and provide £6.3 million for the community over 42 years. The battery will store surplus solar power and release it in the evening when demand is higher, increasing the plant's income.

The site is low-grade agricultural land next to the Yarnton industrial estate and is not currently farmed. The society said it will plant hedgerows and wildflower meadows and add bird and bat boxes and beehives. The name comes from an old Yarnton custom in which villagers drew lots to share out the mowing of their hay meadows.

Since 2012, Low Carbon Hub has built 55 community-owned renewable energy installations across Oxfordshire. Its rooftop solar on 32 schools and 5 businesses saves them around £130,000 a year in energy bills, and it has reinvested more than £2 million of surplus in local communities. As of March 2026, 1,767 investor members had put in more than £10 million. The society signed an agreement to buy the site in September and plans to start construction in May 2027 and begin generating in February 2028.

Citizen Energy View

Common Lot takes investments from £100 to produce electricity for more than 3,000 homes and plans to leave £6.3 million in the community over 42 years. The same society's school rooftop solar, which saves 32 schools around £130,000 a year, shows that community ownership can also help the finances of public facilities. Designing solar and storage together from the start and selling power in the evening is an approach worth considering for our village plants facing curtailment and grid congestion. Villages and local governments preparing Sunlight Income Villages should weigh a community investment model with batteries from the planning stage.
