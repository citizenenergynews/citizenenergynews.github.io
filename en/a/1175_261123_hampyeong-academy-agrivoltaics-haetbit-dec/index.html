Hampyeong holds 8 local talks on solar income villages and agrivoltaics in December

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.23

Hampyeong County will tour four areas from December 1 to 8 with three-hour lectures and consultations. Residents can sign up online, first come first served, until November 30, whether or not they have applied for a project.

Hampyeong County in South Jeolla will run the "Visiting Hampyeong Academy," a series of talks on agrivoltaics (solar panels over farmland) and solar income villages, from December 1 to 8, dividing its nine towns and townships into four areas. Each area gets two sessions, eight in total, and each session lasts three hours: a two-hour lecture and a one-hour consultation. The county's Population and Economy Division posted the notice and a web poster with the schedule for each area on the county website on November 19.

The first session covers agrivoltaics and the second covers solar income villages, from the basics to the current status, key issues, difficulties and good practices. Two experts from the Green Energy Institute, a foundation, will teach. Any Hampyeong resident can attend, whether or not they have applied for a project, and each session takes about 30 to 50 people. Sign-up runs until November 30, first come first served, through the QR code on the poster in the website's notice section, and the division's education support team handles inquiries.

The setback ordinance that the Hampyeong County Council announced on November 17 bars solar within 200 meters of places with five or more houses but exempts facilities selected for resident-income renewable energy programs. Solar built by registered farm and fishery operators within 5 kilometers of their address, and resident-participation facilities, are also exempt (the notice period runs until November 23).

Hakgyo and Eomda townships (area 3) will hold their talks from 2 p.m. to 5 p.m. on December 1 and 8 at the Haknarae Culture and Welfare Center. Nasan, Haebo and Wolya townships (area 4) will meet on the same days from 9 a.m. to noon in the first-floor hall of the Dalmaji Culture Center. Hampyeong-eup and Daedong township (area 1) will meet from 9 a.m. to noon on December 2 and 7 in the multipurpose hall of the Daedong Rest Center. Sonbul and Singwang townships (area 2) will meet on the same days from 2 p.m. to 5 p.m. in the second-floor conference room of the Singwang township office. The last talks are the solar income village sessions for areas 3 and 4 on December 8.

Citizen Energy View

Solar income villages are projects in which residents themselves become the operators and share the revenue, so the chance to hear how they work has to be close to each village. Eight talks with up to 50 people each means at most 400 seats in a county of about 29,000 people. Since the council's draft ordinance exempts resident-income programs from setback limits, village-level consultation should continue after the talks so that residents can step forward before outside developers do. Hampyeong residents who are interested should sign up before the November 30 deadline.
