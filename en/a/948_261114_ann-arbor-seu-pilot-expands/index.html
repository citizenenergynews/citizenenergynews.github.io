Ann Arbor's city-run solar-and-battery utility widens to income-eligible households, with 20 more sites about to go live

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.14

After equipping more than 90 Bryant-area homes, the SEU moves to its next phase; the city plans to open it to all residents and businesses in 2027

The City of Ann Arbor, Michigan, has widened enrolment in its city-run Sustainable Energy Utility (SEU) to residents who meet income criteria. The SEU has installed rooftop solar and batteries on more than 90 homes around Stone School and Ellsworth in the Bryant neighborhood, and 20 more sites will go live soon. SEU Executive Director Shoshannah Lenski said the service is helping households have "more stable, more affordable energy bills" and keeps working through outages.

The SEU, which Ann Arbor voters authorized in November 2024, made its first home installation in the Bryant neighborhood on May 18, and a city press release named about 100 homes in the neighborhood as installation targets. Customers stay connected to the existing grid (the SEU is an optional, supplemental service and does not replace the grid), and the pilot receives grants from the Michigan Public Service Commission and the Michigan Department of Labor and Economic Opportunity.

The city's rate sheet (residential tariff P-1) sets $25 a month from October through March and $75 a month from April through September, with the DTE utility bill paid separately, but the details of the income criteria do not appear in the city materials this newspaper reviewed. The city plans to open the SEU to all residents and businesses in 2027, and residential requests are accepted until before March 1, 2028 (the executive director can adjust that date).
