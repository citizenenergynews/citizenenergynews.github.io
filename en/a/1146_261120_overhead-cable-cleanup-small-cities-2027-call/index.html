Korea invites smaller local governments to bid for overhead cable clean-up

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.20

The science and ICT ministry is accepting applications until 4 December from local governments with up to 500,000 residents. Ten will each get about KRW 2 billion of cable work paid for by KEPCO and telecom operators.

Local governments with 500,000 residents or fewer will have a wider chance to clear tangled power lines and telecom cables from utility poles at the expense of Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) and broadcasting and telecom operators. The Ministry of Science and ICT is inviting basic local governments to apply for the 2027 overhead cable clean-up programme from 20 November to 4 December. Around 130 basic local governments not yet in the programme are eligible, and 10 will be chosen, each receiving clean-up work worth about KRW 2 billion.

KEPCO and the broadcasting and telecom operators, which are obliged to carry out the work, pay the full cost, so the selected local governments do not spend their own budgets. Until now the programme has focused on large cities with more than 500,000 residents. Under the Third Mid- to Long-Term Overhead Cable Clean-up Plan (2026-2030), the ministry will select 10 small and medium-sized local governments every year until 2030 and support one year of work in each.

Applicants must designate at least five areas in urgent need of clean-up and submit a plan. Each area must be no larger than 100,000 square metres and contain no more than 100 power and telecom poles. The review covers the feasibility of the plan, the suitability of the chosen areas, commitment to carrying out the work and the operation of a support system. Applications are received by email at the overhead cable clean-up support centre of the Korea Telecommunications Operators Association, and a briefing for local officials will be held on 26 November.

Results will be announced by official letter in December. Because the programme runs one year at a time, the 10 local governments selected in the 2026 round and still working until the end of this year - Gangneung, Gyeongju, Gimcheon, Namwon, Sejong, Andong, Uijeongbu, Damyang, Jinan and Hampyeong - may reapply on the same terms. This year's national plan covers 130,910 poles (98,805 KEPCO poles and 32,105 telecom poles) in 407 areas of 63 local governments, including Seoul's 25 districts, at a cost of KRW 609.8 billion. From this year, the complaint rate (10%) has been added to household numbers and the number of old houses when allocating work.

Citizen Energy View

Work on 98,805 KEPCO poles is more than tidying wires; it is a chance to look again at local distribution grids. Small and medium-sized towns are also where Sunlight Income Villages and resident-owned solar are being built. If areas with community power plants and shared village facilities are considered when choosing clean-up zones, safety and renewable connection can be handled together. Before the 4 December deadline, county offices and villages should pick priority areas together.
