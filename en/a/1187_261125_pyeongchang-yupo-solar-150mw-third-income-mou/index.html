Pyeongchang Signs Third Income Deal, a 150 MW Solar Park in Yupo-ri

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.25

Pyeongchang County signs an agreement with Jeongsang Group on November 26 for a solar park in Bongpyeong-myeon. Commercial operation is targeted for 2029, together with the Undu-ryeong wind farm.

Pyeongchang County in Gangwon Province will sign a memorandum of understanding (MOU) with Jeongsang Group on November 26 to build a 150 MW solar park around Yupo-ri in Bongpyeong-myeon. In a press release on the 25th, the county said it is the third agreement under its "renewable energy income program," which returns power-generation revenue to residents. The county plans to use income from its stakes in generation projects to fund a basic income and welfare for residents.

The agreement was originally set for November 5 but was postponed because of internal circumstances at a participating organization. In the meantime, the county signed the Undu-ryeong wind agreement on October 28 and a public-led onshore wind agreement on November 18. The Undu-ryeong wind farm is 21 MW, and its agreement was signed by Pyeongchang County, Undu-ryeong Wind Power, Hanuri Power, Korea South-East Power and residents' representatives. The county said the project will follow the development model of the Gadeoksan wind farm in Taebaek, which it cited as a resident-participation model. Under that model, residents' representatives join talks from the start to make the project more transparent, and power revenue is shared with the community.

Both the Yupo-ri solar plant and the Undu-ryeong wind farm aim to begin commercial operation in 2029. The county will look for unused county-owned land to secure more solar plants and will study whether wind farms whose permits were granted and later revoked can be redeveloped. Through this, it plans to further increase its stakes in power plants used to redistribute income to residents by 2035. The press release, however, did not give the total cost of the Yupo-ri project, the county's stake or how much each resident would receive.

County Governor Sim Jae-guk said he would make sure that "the profits of renewable energy development are not monopolized by a particular company but return as benefits to all residents." The signing ceremony will be held at 4 p.m. on November 26 in the governor's office.

Citizen Energy View

Together, the three agreements Pyeongchang is signing this autumn come to 371 MW: 200 MW of onshore wind, 21 MW at Undu-ryeong and 150 MW of solar at Yupo-ri. Following the public-led 200 MW wind agreement this paper reported on November 16, the county's plan to hold stakes in solar as well can answer the worry that development profits flow only to outside companies. But without a fixed county stake and clear rules for sharing with residents, the income program will exist in name only. Before commercial operation begins in 2029, the county should set its stake and distribution rules in an ordinance and open a way for residents to invest directly.
