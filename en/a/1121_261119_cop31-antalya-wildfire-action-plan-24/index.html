Wildfire action plan launched at COP31 with backing from 24 countries

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.19

The Korea Forest Service launched the Antalya Action Plan on Integrated Fire Management with Türkiye and Brazil at the climate summit. Canada, the UK and Japan were among supporters, and the second phase of a joint project with the FAO was presented.

The Antalya Action Plan on Integrated Fire Management, under which countries work together against large wildfires, was launched at the 31st UN Climate Change Conference (COP31). Korea, Türkiye and Brazil jointly led the voluntary international initiative, and the Korea Forest Service said on 19 November that 24 countries besides Korea, including Canada, the UK, the Netherlands and Japan, had backed it. The conference opened on 9 November in Antalya, Türkiye.

The plan aims to share experience and technology between countries and build capacity across the whole cycle from wildfire prevention to preparedness, response and recovery. A high-level dialogue for the launch was held on the 16th (local time), and participating countries agreed to expand cooperation on technology transfer and training and to keep sharing experience. "With climate change raising the risk of large wildfires, joint action by the international community matters more than anything," said Lee Hyunju, acting director general of the Korea Forest Service's International Affairs Bureau.

Earlier, on the 14th, the Korea Forest Service stressed international wildfire cooperation in the face of the climate crisis at a ministerial meeting of the Forest and Climate Leaders' Partnership (FCLP). About 40 minister-level figures attended the meeting. The partnership is an intergovernmental body launched in 2022 to halt global forest loss and land degradation and restore forest ecosystems by 2030. At the meeting the service presented results of its wildfire cooperation with France and the Asian Forest Cooperation Organization (AFoCO). It also shared its experience of working with the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and others.

On the 18th, a side event co-hosted by the FAO and Brazilian civil society groups discussed spreading integrated fire management that links science, finance and local communities. The second phase of AFFIRM (Assuring the Future of Forests with Integrated Risk Management), a joint wildfire cooperation project of the Korea Forest Service and the FAO, was also presented to the international community there. The second phase began in October this year.

Citizen Energy View

Just as the FAO side event treated local communities as one pillar of fire management, residents living near mountains are the first to suffer from wildfires. If wood residues from forest thinning are used as village heating fuel, and villages near forests take on both the energy transition and wildfire prevention, they can gain both monitoring and income. Having helped lead an international action plan, Korea should also widen the way for residents to take part in drawing up wildfire prevention plans at home.
