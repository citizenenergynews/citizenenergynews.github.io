Port of Hueneme switches on 913 kW solar carport, with a 20-year power contract and no installation cost

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.14

More than 1.4 million kWh a year, enough for 250 homes, under the port's goal of zero-emission operations by 2030

The Port of Hueneme, a public port in California, announced on Oct. 30 that it has put a 913 kW solar carport into operation. Built by developer ForeFront Power over a vehicle staging area close to the port's headquarters building, the system produces more than 1.4 million kWh a year, the amount used by 250 homes in the region. It cuts 730 tonnes of carbon dioxide a year, equal to what 170 gasoline passenger cars emit in a year.

The project is structured as a 20-year power purchase agreement (a contract under which the developer owns the system and sells the electricity). ForeFront Power finances, owns and operates the system, and the port pays a fixed rate lower than the utility's. The port paid no installation cost and used no bond funds, and electricity it does not use goes to the local grid.

At the Port of Hueneme, which is run by five port commissioners elected by local residents, Jason Hodge, president of the Oxnard Harbor District Board of Directors, said, "The Port of Hueneme strives to be a leader in maritime sustainability." On June 11, 2024, at a World Oceans Day event, the port unveiled a blueprint to make its operations zero-emission by 2030.
