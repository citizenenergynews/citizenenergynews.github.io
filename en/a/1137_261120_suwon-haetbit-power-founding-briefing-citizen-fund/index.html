Suwon solar company targets 5–8% annual dividend for citizen fund

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.20

A briefing on a 20MW solar company for public parking lots in Suwon was held at the city council on November 16. Under the plan, Suwon Urban Corporation would hold 45%, with cooperatives and local firms taking 20–30% each.

"Suwon Haetbit Power Co.," which is to build about 20MW of solar on public parking lots in downtown Suwon, has set a target of paying its citizen fund a dividend of around 5–8% a year. The Suwon Citizens' Haetbit Power Social Cooperative said on its website on the 19th that a founding briefing for the company was held on the afternoon of November 16 in the first-floor multipurpose hall of the Suwon Special City Council. About 60 cooperative members and residents attended.

Under the plan presented, Suwon Urban Corporation would hold the largest stake at 45%, while local energy cooperatives and companies based in Suwon would each take 20–30%. Residents would join through a separate citizen fund, raised as non-voting preferred shares and paying dividends from power sales. The cooperative described the company's defining feature as public bodies, citizens, cooperatives and businesses building power plants together inside a single corporation, and said this is the first time solar on downtown public parking lots is being pursued this way in Korea.

Attendees asked for a separate channel through which citizens could feel they own the project, rather than stopping at dividend-only investment without votes. They also asked how financial stability would be secured and whether existing cooperative activities would shrink once the company is formed. Some said Suwon Urban Corporation should join the discussion even before the company is set up.

In the panel discussion, the chair of Suwon Energy Cooperative said her cooperative intends to invest and proposed building an "urban sunlight income village" that links energy education and local welfare. The secretary-general of Gongdong-ui Jip Energy Cooperative called for an open recruitment of the chief executive and a democratic board, and urged preparing financial measures and policy financing in advance so that contributions are not delayed during staged capital increases. The head of the Jeonju Carbon Neutrality Support Center said the department in charge should keep the project going while making use of the advantages of integrated management. In a notice on the 2nd, the cooperative had said that Suwon City and local energy cooperatives prepared the company to expand solar power plants in 2027. The cooperative said it will communicate fully with citizens and go through a public deliberation process before the company is founded.

Citizen Energy View

If the citizen fund is raised only as non-voting preferred shares, citizens remain investors who collect dividends. In a cooperative, every member has one vote regardless of how much they invest, and in the new company that voice rests on the cooperatives' 20–30% share. That is why how the articles of incorporation handle the recruitment of the chief executive, the makeup of the board and channels for citizen participation matters as much as the dividend rate. More Suwon residents should come to the public discussions before the founding and help settle these terms together.
