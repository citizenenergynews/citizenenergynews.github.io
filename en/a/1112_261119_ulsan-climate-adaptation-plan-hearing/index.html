Ulsan's 2027-2031 climate adaptation draft has 6 sectors and 20 tasks, with a public briefing on Nov. 24 to collect views

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.19

Core projects include flood control at Hoeya Dam and heat protection for vulnerable residents; the draft lists 74 detailed projects in total / Sector-by-sector counts add up to 73, and the city says the plan may still change

Ulsan has published the draft of its "4th Ulsan Metropolitan City Climate Crisis Adaptation Plan" for the five years from 2027 to 2031, and will hold a public briefing at 2 p.m. on Nov. 24 in the Citizens' Hall of City Hall (first floor of the city council building). The draft is organised into 20 tasks across six sectors: water management, forests and ecosystems, land and coasts, agriculture and fisheries, health, and mainstreaming adaptation (building climate adaptation into all areas of administration).

The city gives the total number of detailed projects as 74, but the sector figures (water management 11, forests and ecosystems 18, land and coasts 9, agriculture and fisheries 15, health 18, mainstreaming 2) add up to 73. Core projects include strengthening flood control at Hoeya Dam, building an AI-based system for running the water supply and responding to crises, operating disaster warning systems, stronger protection for people vulnerable to heatwaves, and a survey of climate-vulnerable residents with adaptation support.

The briefing is open to any resident and will run for an hour, covering an explanation of the plan, questions and answers, and the collection of opinions. The city takes registrations and comments through a survey linked by a QR code on its notice, and says the draft is still under consultation and may change.

Citizen Energy View

Protecting vulnerable people from heatwaves eventually comes down to whether they can afford the electricity bill for cooling. Yet none of the draft's six sectors is dedicated to energy. Putting solar panels co-owned by residents on the roofs of public buildings and rental housing, and spending the income on cooling costs for vulnerable households, would tackle adaptation and emissions cuts at the same time. With the draft still under consultation, the Nov. 24 briefing and the survey are the place to ask for that link.
