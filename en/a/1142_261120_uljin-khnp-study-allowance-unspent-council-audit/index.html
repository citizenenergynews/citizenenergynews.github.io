Uljin's KHNP-funded programs leave over 9% unspent each year

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.20

Of about 2.09 billion won in Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power support for Uljin in 2025, about 190 million went unspent. The North Gyeongsang council asked for tighter use rules and checks on overlap.

Support programs in Uljin funded by Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power (KHNP) have left more than 9% of their budgets unspent each year. Kim Sang-il (Pohang 3), a member of the North Gyeongsang Provincial Council's education committee, raised the issue on the 13th during an audit of the Yeongju, Bonghwa and Uljin education support offices, and also questioned how a study allowance for students run under these programs is being used. The council made the details public on the 16th.

According to data from the North Gyeongsang Office of Education, KHNP support programs in Uljin totalled about 1.79 billion won in 2024 and about 2.09 billion won in 2025. Unspent amounts were about 160 million won (9.2%) in 2024 and about 190 million won (9.4%) in 2025. Kim asked whether the demand surveys and plans had been flawed from the start, and called for improvement, saying it was hard to understand why the education office left close to 10% of outside funding unspent every year when it struggles even to obtain budget from the provincial office.

Because the study allowance can be used at most places in the area, Kim said the education support office should have some idea of whether it is being spent at places unrelated to study, such as internet cafes. Kim also asked the office to consider limiting where it can be used to study-related items when it next consults with KHNP.

Uljin, home to nuclear power plants, receives local resource and facility tax. The Local Tax Act levies 1 won per kWh generated on nuclear power and 0.7 won on thermal power (0.6 won for liquefied natural gas). Kim explained that Uljin's revenue from this tax is estimated at about 35.5 billion won, of which 65%, about 23.1 billion won, goes to Uljin County, yet the county's fiscal self-reliance ratio stays at around 12%. Kim asked the head of the education office to check whether elementary and early-childhood programs run by the office and by Uljin County overlap, and to hold an education administration council at least once a quarter so budgets are used efficiently.

Citizen Energy View

Uljin receives local resource and facility tax estimated at about 35.5 billion won and KHNP program funds in the range of 2 billion won, yet more than 9% of those program funds go unspent every year. That is not a sign of too little money but a sign that demand surveys and planning need to be revisited. Since these funds belong to residents who live alongside nuclear plants, parents and residents should help decide how they are used. The education support office and Uljin County should publish each year how much is left over and where the money goes, so residents can check.
