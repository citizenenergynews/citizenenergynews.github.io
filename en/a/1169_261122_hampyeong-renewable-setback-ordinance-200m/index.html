Hampyeong draft setback ordinance sets 200m from homes for solar

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.22

Hampyeong County Council's draft sets solar at 200m and wind at 1,500m, the maximum the decree allows, while exempting resident-participation and public-contest projects. Comments close November 23.

The Hampyeong County Council in South Jeolla Province has given notice of a draft ordinance on setback standards for renewable energy facilities that would bar solar plants within a 200m radius, and wind turbines within 1,500m, of places with five or more houses. The council issued Notice No. 2026-11 on November 17 and is taking comments from November 18 to 23. Six cases, including resident-participation power facilities and rooftop and self-consumption solar, would be exempt from the setback limits.

The draft is based on the Renewable Energy Development, Use and Promotion Act, which took effect on September 18, and Article 27-3 of its enforcement decree. Under the amended law, local governments may set setback distances by ordinance only within the range set by law. The decree caps what ordinances may require at 200m from homes for solar, and at 1,500m from homes and 500m from certain roads for wind; all three figures in Hampyeong's draft match those caps.

Article 4 of the draft bars renewable energy facilities within 500m of historic and cultural environment conservation areas and protection zones, ecological and landscape conservation areas, tourist sites and complexes, and natural village districts. Wind turbines also may not be built within a 500m radius of roads. Distances are measured from the boundary of the plant site, and the scope of houses and roads follows a notice by the Minister of Climate, Energy and Environment.

Beyond resident-participation facilities, rooftop solar and self-consumption solar, the draft lists three more exemptions (Article 6). They cover cases where all owners and resident heads of household of homes within 200m consent, and projects selected in national or local government contests for resident-income renewable energy. A registered farm or fishery business operator installing solar on a site within 5km of their registered address is also exempt. Comments can be submitted to the council's policy support team by November 23 in writing, in person or by fax.

Citizen Energy View

Hampyeong's draft sets restrictions at the full distance the decree allows, while exempting entirely projects in which residents take part or that win resident-income contests. On the same land, an outside developer acting alone would be blocked, while a project owned by residents would be allowed. Because the definition of "resident participation" simply follows Article 27-3(3) of the law, however, the county also needs a way to check how large residents' stakes and profit shares really are if it is to screen out projects that only borrow the name. With comments closing on November 23, villages and local cooperatives need to speak up quickly.
