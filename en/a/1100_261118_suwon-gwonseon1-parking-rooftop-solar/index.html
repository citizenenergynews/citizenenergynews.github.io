Suwon's Gwonseon 1-dong public car park puts 44.64kW of solar on a roof over its top deck

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.18

Five-storey, 122-space car park on a former community centre site cost 6.7 billion won / City estimates about 12 million won a year in power savings; free trial from Nov. 23

Suwon Special City built a five-storey public car park with room for 122 vehicles on the site of the former Gwonseon 1-dong administrative welfare centre (1166 Gwonseon-dong, Gwonseon-gu) and held a completion ceremony on Nov. 17. It is the first of Suwon's public car parks to put a roof over its rooftop parking deck, and that roof carries a 44.64kW solar power system.

The city estimates the system will cut carbon emissions by 25 to 30 tonnes and save about 12 million won in electricity costs each year. The car park has a total floor area of 4,507.03 square metres; construction began in July 2025 and finished in October 2026, at a total cost of 6.7 billion won.

Suwon Urban Corporation will run it free of charge as a trial from Nov. 23 to Dec. 13 and start charging on Dec. 14. The first hour is free, then 300 won per 10 minutes, up to 7,000 won a day; monthly passes are planned at 60,000 won for daytime, 40,000 won for night-time and 70,000 won for all day.

Citizen Energy View

If the city's estimate holds, a 44.64kW system on a single roof will trim about 12 million won from the power bill every year. Article 12-13 of the Act on the Promotion of the Development, Use and Diffusion of Renewable Energy requires local-government car parks with 1,000 square metres or more of parking area to install at least 1kW of renewable capacity per 10 square metres of effective parking area, making renewable installations mandatory at public car parks above that threshold. Eumseong County in North Chungcheong, which this paper reported on Nov. 15, has decided to spend car-park solar revenue on support for energy-poor households and resident welfare. Suwon should open the roofs of its remaining public car parks to citizen-funded cooperatives so that the profit from power made on public land goes back to residents.
