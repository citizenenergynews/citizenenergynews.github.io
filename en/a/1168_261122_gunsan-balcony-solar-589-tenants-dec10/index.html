Gunsan balcony solar for 589 homes opens to tenants until December 10

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.22

Gunsan has extended applications to December 10 for home solar units, covering 800,000 won of the 1 million won cost of a 500 W system. Apartment complexes can apply as a group.

Gunsan in North Jeolla has extended the application period for its household (balcony) solar program to December 10 and widened eligibility so that tenants with the owner's consent can also apply. The city issued a notice amending its subsidy plan (No. 2026-2587) on November 18. The original deadline was September 30, and only homeowners were eligible. The change follows the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment's amended notices on household solar support (No. 2026-960 and No. 2026-1001).

The program covers 589 households, with installation planned during 2027. Each household can install up to 500 W of solar, and the city pays a flat 800,000 won of the 1 million won installation cost for a 500 W system (400,000 won from the national government, 40,000 won from the province and 360,000 won from the city). Applicants pay 200,000 won. Tenants must submit documents proving the lease and either the owner's consent to installation or a pledge to restore the property, and apartment buildings must submit the consent of the management body.

Multiple units in the same apartment complex, or several units owned by a corporation or institution, can be combined into a single application per complex. In that case applicants submit a list of participating units and a resolution of the residents' representative council, and an evaluation committee may adjust the national subsidy taking into account cost savings from group installation and site conditions. For group applications, the self-paid portion can be paid in instalments under a separate agreement if the applicants and the installer agree.

Applicants choose a registered installer on the Korea Energy Agency's Green Home website and sign a contract directly, and the installer submits the documents to the city. After installation, once Gunsan and the agency's renewable energy centre have inspected the site, the applicant pays the self-paid portion to the installer and the city pays the subsidy to the installer. Systems installed by non-registered companies are not eligible for the subsidy.

Citizen Energy View

Opening applications to tenants who do not own their homes widens balcony solar from a benefit for landlords to a choice for everyone who lives in a home. With a 500 W system costing residents 200,000 won, group applications by apartment complexes and instalment payments give residents' councils a route to organise energy projects for a whole complex. However, the program is capped at 589 households and installation will not happen until 2027, so some households may have to wait if demand is high. Gunsan residents and apartment communities should consider putting a complex-wide application on their residents' council agenda before the December 10 deadline.
