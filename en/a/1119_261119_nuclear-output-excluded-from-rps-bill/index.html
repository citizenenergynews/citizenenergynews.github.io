Korean bill would exclude nuclear output when calculating renewable supply obligations; 12 lawmakers' amendment open for comment until Nov. 22

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.19

Mandatory renewable supply is set yearly by presidential decree at up to 25% of total generation / The bill aims to ease nuclear operators' burden of buying supply certificates and would take effect six months after promulgation

A group of 12 Korean lawmakers on Nov. 11 filed an amendment to the Act on the Promotion of the Development, Use and Diffusion of Renewable Energy that would exclude electricity from nuclear power when calculating mandatory renewable energy supply. The bill was referred to the National Assembly's Climate, Energy, Environment and Labor Committee on Nov. 12, and the Assembly's public notice period runs from Nov. 13 to 22.

Current law requires power generators above a certain size to supply renewable energy in proportion to their total generation, or to meet the obligation by buying renewable energy certificates (RECs). The total mandatory supply is set each year by presidential decree at up to 25% of total generation (Article 12-5, Paragraph 2). For renewable sources that need balanced deployment, part of the total obligation can be required to come from that source. Nuclear generators carry the same obligation as other generators.

The amendment would remove electricity produced by operating power reactors under the Nuclear Safety Act from "total generation" in that provision. The sponsors said nuclear power is a carbon-free source that emits no greenhouse gases, yet it bears the same certificate-purchase obligation as fossil fuel generation, adding to the industry's burden. The law would take effect six months after promulgation and apply from the first calculation of suppliers' total obligations after it takes effect. Comments can be submitted on the National Assembly's legislative notice website until Nov. 22.

According to the Korea Energy Agency's New and Renewable Energy Center, obligated suppliers under the renewable portfolio standard (RPS) are generators that own 500 MW or more of generating capacity, excluding renewable facilities. As of January 2026, there were 29 obligated suppliers, including Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power. Each supplier's obligation is calculated by multiplying its total generation (excluding renewable generation) by the annual obligation ratio, which is 15.0% for 2026.

Citizen Energy View

Removing nuclear output from the calculation would shrink mandatory supply by that amount and reduce demand for certificates along with it. Cooperatives and village solar projects that have relied on certificate sales are the operators most vulnerable to such changes. If obligations are to be eased because nuclear is carbon-free, the debate should also address how to protect the revenue of small plants owned by local residents. We hope citizen power producers will assess the bill's impact and submit comments during the notice period.
