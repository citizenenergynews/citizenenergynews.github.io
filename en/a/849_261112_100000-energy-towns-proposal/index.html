Emeritus professor Hwang Woo-hyun proposes 100,000 self-reliant sun-and-wind energy towns across Korea

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.12

1MW per town and a cumulative 100GW by 2035; review of a 5,700-household complex in Namyangju finds energy self-sufficiency of about 45% to 80%

Hwang Woo-hyun, emeritus professor at Seoul National University of Science and Technology, proposed in a November 12 special contribution to Korea Electric Power News that 'self-reliant sun-and-wind energy towns' be built in 60,000 urban neighbourhood units (tong) and 40,000 rural villages (ri). Each town would host 1MW of solar, wind, electric vehicles and similar assets, reaching 30,000 towns and 30GW in 2030 and a cumulative 100,000 towns and 100GW in 2035. The first step would start in 2027, with 242 local governments each running about 12 pilots, roughly 3,000 towns (3GW) in all.

Apartment complexes as the main site Hwang noted that 21,678 apartment complexes hold 12.33 million homes and 30.19 million people, about 58% of the population. He said he reviewed a 5,700-household complex in Dasan-dong, Namyangju, at the request of a citizens' environmental group, and that adding small wind, elevator regenerative braking, energy storage (ESS) and two-way vehicle charging gives self-sufficiency of about 45% to 80%. Project financing would cover 85% of costs, and local government, the project operator and residents would share the rest by stake. With an average 1MW across about 20,000 complexes at a 14% capacity factor, output would be about 25TWh a year, roughly 5% of national electricity consumption.

Grid bottlenecks and jobs The piece cites the Bukdangjin–Sintangjeong transmission line, delayed by more than 150 months, as the reason to generate close to where power is used. His calculation is that two specialist small firms per town, hiring three people each, would create 600,000 jobs and allow a share of 600,000 to 1 million won per household a year.

How it differs from the government plan Hwang cited the government's 2030 target for sun-and-wind income villages as 3,000 and compared it with his proposal of 100,000. That is about 33 times 3,000. The plan the government announced after the March 24 cabinet report is to select at least 500 villages this year and build at least 2,500 by 2030, with at least 10 residents forming a cooperative to build and run a solar plant on idle village land.
