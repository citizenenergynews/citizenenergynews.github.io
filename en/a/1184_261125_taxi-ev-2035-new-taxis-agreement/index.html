Korea Taxi Pact Aims for All-Electric New Taxis by 2035

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.25

Electric cabs make up 17% of Korea's 229,000 taxis. Two ministries and two taxi federations signed up to a five-year pact on chargers, loans and switching subsidies.

The South Korean government and the taxi industry have set a goal that, in principle, every taxi newly bought or leased from 2035 onward will be electric. The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment, the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport, the National Taxi Transport Business Association and the National Private Taxi Transport Business Association said on November 24 that they would sign a "business agreement to promote taxi electrification" that afternoon at the National Land Development Exhibition Hall in Jung-gu, Seoul. The four bodies will meet regularly to discuss the direction of the switch.

About 229,000 taxis were registered nationwide in the first half of this year, of which about 39,000 (17%) were electric. Over the past three years an average of about 35,000 taxis were newly registered each year. By the industry's count, a taxi travels 5.8 times (private cabs) to 7.3 times (company cabs) as far as an ordinary passenger car. The two ministries say this is why replacing a single taxi cuts so much greenhouse gas and exhaust.

Under the agreement, the climate ministry will develop policies for the switch and for easier charging and provide administrative and financial support. The attached event plan lists examples such as more fast chargers at depots, loans for company-owned electric taxis and switching subsidies, and names EV charging subsidies as the transport ministry's part. The company-taxi association will set up operating systems suited to electric cabs, while the private-taxi association will help drivers understand safe operation and change attitudes.

This year an electric taxi receives 2.5 million won on top of the maximum national subsidy for mid-size and large electric passenger cars. A switching subsidy created this year, paid when a buyer scraps or sells a combustion car at least three years old and buys an EV, is worth up to 1 million won. The agreement is valid for five years from signing and stays in force during that period unless one party gives written notice of termination.

Citizen Energy View

A taxi runs through the city all day, so replacing one has the effect of replacing several private cars. The industry's worries about vehicle prices and charging time will ease only when the support written into the agreement, such as depot fast chargers and company loans, turns into real budgets. But if the charging power comes from coal and gas, the emissions cut is only half done. Let us put solar panels jointly owned by residents and cooperatives on the roofs of taxi depots and public charging stations, so that taxis run on electricity made in their own communities.
