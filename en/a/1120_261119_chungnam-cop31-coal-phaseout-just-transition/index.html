Chungnam shares coal exit and just transition at COP31

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.19

South Chungcheong Province presented its closure of three coal units and its transition fund in Antalya, Türkiye, on Nov. 11-18. A support act takes effect on Sept. 16, 2027.

South Chungcheong Province (Chungnam) presented its experience of closing coal-fired power plants and pursuing a "just transition" at the 31st UN climate conference (COP31). The province's director-general for climate, environment and forests visited Antalya, Türkiye, where the conference was held, from November 11 to 18. The province said on the 19th that the director-general discussed responses to the climate crisis with international climate bodies and government officials.

The main presentation came on the 14th, at a "From Chungnam to Asia" session of a forum hosted by Solutions for Our Climate. The province described how it closed three coal-fired units in 2020 and 2025. It also explained how it set up a just transition fund to limit the impact on workers' jobs and the local economy, and how it pressed for a special act. The province said central government institutional and financial support is important for fostering replacement industries and shifting jobs. On the 13th, at a high-level discussion hosted by a Kyrgyz climate finance center and ICLEI East Asia, it introduced its refrigerant management and methane reduction policies. At a meeting with the head of the Powering Past Coal Alliance secretariat on the 16th, the two sides agreed to support each other's capacity for just transition policies.

In Korea, the Special Act on Support for Coal-Fired Power Plant Workers and Closure Areas (Act No. 21942) was promulgated on September 15. Power generators must submit a closure plan, including job security plans for workers and the results of consultations with residents, to the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment two years before the planned closure date. Provincial governors must set up a regional transition council with generators, resident representatives, worker representatives and experts, which reviews the annual implementation plan. The state and local governments may give priority support to renewable energy deployment projects in closure areas and may lease state- and publicly owned property to replacement businesses through private contracts. The act takes effect on September 16, 2027, and its support provisions also apply to areas where plants stopped operating before then under the 11th Basic Plan for Electricity Supply and Demand.

Citizen Energy View

Chungnam's experience of closing three coal units early shows that a coal exit does not end when the plant gates shut. For residents worried about jobs and the local economy, the most convincing answer is to reuse plant sites and grid connections for renewable energy and keep the profits in the region. Since the new act puts resident representatives on regional transition councils and gives priority support to renewables, its enforcement decree should include criteria that favor replacement projects in which residents and workers invest. The ten months left before the act takes effect should be used to prepare, so that the experience Chungnam shared abroad leads to a transition owned by residents.
