Korea proposes grid assessment waiver for AI data centres of 50MW or less

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.21

Facilities in special zones would skip the non-technical review and face only the technical one. Comments on the draft decree are due 30 December, with a public hearing on 9 December.

Artificial intelligence (AI) data centres built outside the Seoul metropolitan area with a contracted capacity of 50MW or less would no longer need a power grid impact assessment under a proposal now under way. The Ministry of Science and ICT on 20 November gave legislative notice of draft enforcement decree and rules for the Special Act on the Promotion of the AI Data Centre Industry containing this threshold. On the same day, the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment and the science ministry said they would also reform the system to simplify assessments for data centres in special zones.

The two ministries weighed that even a 100MW threshold would leave most gigawatt-scale data centres in mega-projects outside the exemption. They also took into account that developers spend much of their time preparing for the non-technical review. As a result, facilities in AI data centre special zones under the act would be exempt from the non-technical review regardless of size and would face only the technical review. The climate ministry plans to amend its notice on operating the grid impact assessment system for this purpose. It also plans to strengthen links between the two types of zones in line with the act, which ties the special zones to distributed energy special zones (the "demand-attraction" type of distributed energy zone is already exempt from the non-technical review). Data centres in mega-projects and those applying for bundled permit processing would be assessed first regardless of size.

Under the draft, a facility qualifies as an AI data centre if its AI equipment power is 250kW or more and accounts for at least 50% of total ICT equipment power, or if it reaches 5MW or more. To stop developers from filing a construction notice only to lock in privileges such as the assessment waiver, the draft requires them to set and report a construction start date within one year of filing. Local governments applying for a special zone must submit plans on grid hosting capacity within the zone and on local benefit-sharing and resident acceptance, and the science ministry will review them within 90 days.

Comments on the draft can be submitted by email or post until 30 December, and the science ministry will hold a public hearing at Nurikkum Square in Mapo-gu, Seoul, on 9 December. The act takes effect on 10 March 2027, and the government plans to bring the subordinate rules into force on the same day.

Citizen Energy View

Exempting projects of 50MW or less outside the capital region also means that much demand can join local grids without a grid assessment. Since the draft puts resident acceptance plans into special zone applications and the government says it will draw up guidelines on consulting nearby residents in the first half of 2027, those standards must not stay on paper. When data centres run on renewable power owned by residents and cooperatives, benefits beyond the electricity bill stay in the region. Local governments preparing for special zones should draw up a resident-participation generation plan before the application.
