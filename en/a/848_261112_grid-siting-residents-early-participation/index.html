Up to two-thirds of transmission-line support funds to go to nearby areas; resident briefings rise from two to three

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.12

Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment holds public notice until Dec. 1; alternative routes proposed by residents to go before siting committees

Under an amendment the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment put out for legislative and administrative notice on Oct. 22, residents near transmission lines can submit alternative routes or sites in writing. If the siting committee deliberates and approves a proposal, it becomes a candidate site.

Before a committee is formed, the city-, county- and district-level resident briefings rise from two to three, and residents may attend committee meetings in principle. Of the local government support of 2 billion won per kilometre of overhead line, up to two-thirds goes to areas around the facilities, and installation costs are reflected first when a village runs a solar project. As a follow-up to the Sept. 15 report to the Cabinet meeting, pilot 'grid sunlight income villages' (projects through which villages near the grid earn income from solar power) will also be identified with local governments and Korea Electric Power Corp.

Lee Jeong-hyeon, head of the Jeonbuk Environmental Movement Federation, said residents should take part from the early stage when the outlines of substations (points) and transmission lines (lines) are drawn. The ministry plans to take views from residents and local governments until Dec. 1, then complete revisions of subordinate regulations and notices after review by the Ministry of Government Legislation.
