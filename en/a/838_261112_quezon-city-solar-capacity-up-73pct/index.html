Quezon City Solar Up 73% to 58.55MW, but Rooftop Solar Adoption Stays Below 1%

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.12

Installations spread from city hall to public schools and city hospitals; satellite analysis puts rooftop potential at up to 4,916MW

Solar capacity in Quezon City, Metro Manila, is estimated to have grown 73% in under two years to 58.55MW. The figure comes from a November 12 press release by the Institute for Climate and Sustainable Cities (ICSC), a Philippine non-governmental organization, and compares with 33.85MW based on 2023–2024 data. Rooftop systems account for 55.88MW, more than 95% of the total, and produce over 78GWh a year, at least 1.35% of the city's 2025 electricity consumption.

The city began with its city hall buildings and extended solar to public schools and city-owned hospitals, and installations at large malls such as SM, Ayala and Robinsons also added capacity. Even so, rooftop solar adoption remains below 1%, and satellite analysis estimates the city's total rooftop potential at up to 4,916MW.

Monalisa Dimalanta, ICSC senior adviser for energy literacy and energy democracy, said affordable financing and simpler permitting are needed for the benefits to reach households and small businesses that struggle with installation costs. Following its first analysis released in July 2025 (174 cities and municipalities nationwide, 1,846.08MW detected in total), ICSC this time used satellite imagery taken in April 2026.
