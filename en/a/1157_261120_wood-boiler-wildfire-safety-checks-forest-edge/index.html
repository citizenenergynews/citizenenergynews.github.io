Forest Service checks wood-burning boilers near forests in Chungju and Jincheon

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.20

The Central Regional Forest Service and the Chungju National Forest Office are visiting forest-edge homes that burn wood for heat, checking chimneys, extinguishers and ash disposal. The autumn wildfire caution period runs to 15 December.

The Korea Forest Service is visiting homes in farming and mountain villages next to forests, one by one, to check wood-burning boilers. The Chungju National Forest Office said on 19 November that it is carrying out intensive on-site safety checks of households using wood boilers in Chungju, Jincheon and other areas it manages. Focusing on farm households close to forests, it is examining how boilers are installed and maintained, whether flammable materials are piled nearby and how leftover ash is disposed of.

The Central Regional Forest Service also said it had visited households using wood boilers in areas adjoining forests to carry out safety checks. The checks cover four items: whether the boiler is in a suitable place, whether the chimney is properly installed and maintained, whether a fire extinguisher is on hand, and whether ash is disposed of safely after use. The Central Regional Forest Service also explained safety rules to residents and handed out leaflets.

Wood boilers are widely used by rural households trying to save on heating costs, but sparks from chimneys can spread into woodland, and ash that has not fully cooled can start wildfires if dumped carelessly. When it announced its autumn wildfire prevention measures on 28 October, the Forest Service said it would list households using wood boilers in forest-edge areas before winter and inspect on site how they handle chimneys, fuel and ash.

According to the Forest Service, there were about 44 autumn wildfires a year on average over the past 10 years, burning 21ha. By cause, 31% were started accidentally by visitors to the mountains and 14% by open burning. Last autumn, 66% of all wildfires began as fires outside forests that spread through adjoining areas. This year's autumn wildfire caution period runs from 1 November to 15 December. At the same briefing, the Forest Service said that from 13 November the maximum prison term for causing a wildfire through negligence would rise from three to five years, with penalties of up to five years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 50 million. To cut fires from open burning, it starts shredding farm by-products right after the autumn harvest and lends shredders free of charge to farms that want them.

Citizen Energy View

Wood boilers are chosen by rural households burdened by heating oil and electricity costs, yet they leave each home carrying the risk of sparks and ash. Beyond inspections and awareness campaigns, ways should be found to help households next to forests switch to safer heating such as air-source heat pumps or rooftop solar. Using income from village-run solar power to ease heating bills could also be an option. Before the caution period ends on 15 December, villages could start by reviewing their own wood-boiler households.
