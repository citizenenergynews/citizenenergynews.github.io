Gyeonggi completes three urban regeneration hubs in Suwon, Pocheon and Pyeongtaek

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.20

The Seryu Eoullim Center in Suwon, the Gwanin 1+ Center in Pocheon and the Sinpyeong Dagachi Community Center in Pyeongtaek open in November, while work begins on the Jeongok Culture Station in Yeoncheon, due for completion in October 2028.

Three old town centres in Gyeonggi Province are getting shared neighbourhood hubs for residents this month. Gyeonggi Province said on 19 November that three urban regeneration hub facilities in Seryu-dong in Suwon, Gwanin-myeon in Pocheon and Pyeongtaek-dong in Pyeongtaek will be completed in November, and that construction of a fourth will begin in Jeongok-eup in Yeoncheon County. Urban regeneration hubs are spaces built through urban regeneration projects to fill gaps in cultural, sports and care facilities in ageing residential areas and old town centres, and to serve as places for residents to meet and run community activities.

The largest of the completed facilities, the Seryu Eoullim Center in Suwon, has two basement floors and three above-ground floors with a total floor area of 4,587 square metres. It houses sports facilities such as table tennis and pickleball courts, a community childcare centre, a small library and a village cafe, and the public car park has been expanded and fitted with electric vehicle chargers. A 1,454-square-metre residents' yard around the building will be used for gatherings and cultural events.

The Gwanin 1+ Center in Pocheon (three floors, 1,266 square metres) has a village kitchen where residents cook and share food together, a multipurpose hall, a classroom and a cafe. It plans to run health management and dementia prevention activities using digital technology and artificial intelligence, as well as care and education programmes for children and teenagers. The Sinpyeong Dagachi Community Center in Pyeongtaek (two basement floors and four above-ground floors, 4,179 square metres) has 46 public parking spaces underground, while the upper floors will house a space to help residents from multicultural backgrounds settle in and connect, and a centre supporting residents in their 50s and 60s with health, self-development, job seeking and business start-ups.

The Jeongok Culture Station in Yeoncheon, where work starts this month, will be built in the Jeongok-ri area with two basement floors and six above-ground floors and a total floor area of 7,542 square metres. Alongside a children's play facility, a prehistoric culture experience space and a shared kitchen, it will include 20 housing units for young people and 46 parking spaces, with completion targeted for October 2028. Gyeonggi Province said it will continue to support better living conditions and community activities through urban regeneration projects suited to each area.

Citizen Energy View

Shared spaces such as village cafes, village kitchens and residents' yards are good places to start a neighbourhood energy transition. If residents jointly invest in solar panels on the roofs and car parks of public hub facilities, the income can go back into running costs and care programmes. The time to draw up energy plans together with resident operating groups is now, while operating plans are being set, rather than after the buildings are finished. Starting with the three hubs opening this month, residents could set up a regular forum to look at electricity and heating bills together.
