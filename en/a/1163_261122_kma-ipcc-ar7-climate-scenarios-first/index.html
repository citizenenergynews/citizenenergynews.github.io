Korea's first IPCC AR7 climate scenarios project 1.2–3.6°C warming by century's end

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.22

The Korea Meteorological Administration produced two global scenarios for the IPCC Seventh Assessment Report. East Asia warms by up to 4.1°C under high emissions, and a Korean Peninsula standard scenario follows in 2030.

The Korea Meteorological Administration (KMA) said on 20 November that it had produced, for the first time, two global climate change scenarios to be used in the Seventh Assessment Report of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC). Under the high-emissions pathway, global mean temperature in 2081–2100 rises 3.6°C above the present (2002–2021) and precipitation increases 7.4%. Under the very-low-emissions pathway, which stabilises warming at around 1.5°C by 2100, temperature rises 1.2°C and precipitation 3.9% over the same period.

The seven new emissions pathways reflect policies since the Paris Agreement and the actual pace of the energy transition, so even the high-emissions pathway emits less than the worst AR6 pathway (SSP5-8.5). The medium pathway among the seven assumes emissions continue at current (2025) policy levels, with slow but continuing warming. In the AR6 projections (July 2020), SSP5-8.5 showed 5.2°C of warming in the late 21st century, but the reference period (1995–2014) differs, so the figures are hard to compare.

The new runs used K-ACE2, an Earth system model that calculates the atmosphere, ocean, sea ice and land surface as well as the land and ocean carbon cycle. In the emissions-driven mode, which directly calculates carbon emission and uptake, the high-emissions pathway produced 3.8°C of warming and a 7.7% increase in precipitation, slightly higher than the concentration-driven mode. East Asia (10–55°N, 80–150°E) warmed 3.8°C (concentration-driven) and 4.1°C (emissions-driven) under high emissions, more than the global average. The results combine six simulations run from different initial states of the model.

The KMA plans to produce the remaining five scenarios, submit them to the data centre of the seventh Coupled Model Intercomparison Project (CMIP7) and share them with researchers worldwide. The results will also feed into the national standard climate change scenario, which combines multiple scenarios to avoid policy confusion. Within the standard scenario, the Korean Peninsula domain is the most widely used, for regional adaptation plans and sustainability (ESG) disclosure. The standard scenarios will be released for the globe in 2028, East Asia in 2029 and the Korean Peninsula in 2030.

Citizen Energy View

The removal of the worst-case pathway is no reason to relax. Even under the high-emissions pathway, global temperature rises 3.6°C by the end of the century and East Asia up to 4.1°C. What separates 1.2°C from 3.6°C is the emissions from now on, and the fastest way to cut them is renewable energy that villages and cooperatives build themselves on rooftops and idle land. Before the Korean Peninsula scenario arrives in 2030, let us set local adaptation plans and community-owned generation targets together.
