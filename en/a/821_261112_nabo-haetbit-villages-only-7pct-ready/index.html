Only 36 of 523 Sunlight Income Villages Ready to Start; Budget Office Urges Delivery Support First

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.12

Of 87 first-round villages, just 13 can proceed now and 59 were conditionally selected over grid shortfalls / 2027 budget adds 461 million won for the task force's running costs

In its analysis of the Ministry of the Interior and Safety's 2027 budget, the National Assembly Budget Office recommended that the Sunlight Income Village task force put coordination across ministries and support for villages already selected ahead of adding new selections. Of the 523 villages chosen this year, 36 (7%) can start work immediately: 13 (14.9%) of the 87 first-round villages plus 23 (5.2%) of the 436 selected in the second round announced in October. Among first-round villages, 59 were selected conditionally because of inadequate grid capacity, and the other conditional villages were chosen on the premise of fixes such as land lease talks.

The report noted that these constraints depend on policy tools held by agencies such as the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment (the climate ministry), Korea Electric Power Corporation and the Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs, so on-site help from the task force alone cannot resolve them (the task force itself handles the steps from the public call to notifying results, while financing, permits and installation fall to the Korea Energy Agency, financial institutions and local governments). The government amended the Electricity Business Act and the Distributed Energy Activation Special Act to create a basis for priority grid access for resident-participation renewable projects of 1 MW or less. In the 2027 budget, the new Sunlight Income Village expansion item under the interior ministry is 461 million won in running costs for the task force, while project spending on selection, evaluation and education is budgeted separately in the climate ministry's electricity industry fund.

The scope and requirements of priority access will be set by subordinate legislation and agency operating procedures, and the report gives no effective date.

Reading the News The analysis appears on pages 50 to 55 of the Budget Office's "2027 Budget Analysis by Committee: Public Administration and Security Committee" and can be downloaded from its website, nabo.go.kr. According to the report, the interior ministry set targets of 700 villages in 2026, 2,200 in 2027 and at least 3,000 by 2030. On November 12, this paper reported support for conditionally selected villages.
