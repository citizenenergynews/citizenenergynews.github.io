490 of 522 Sunlight Income Villages Selected in Rounds One and Two Are Conditional, and Their Effectiveness Became an Issue in the National Assembly Budget Review

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.10

The Ministry of the Interior and Safety counts 77 conditional villages in round one and 413 in round two; Rep. Lim Ho-seon noted the plan to expand to 2,200 villages next year rests mainly on 28.5 billion won in interest subsidies

Of the 522 villages chosen as Sunlight Income Villages, 86 in the first round and 436 in the second, 490 were selected with conditions attached. The Ministry of the Interior and Safety classified 77 of the 86 first-round villages as conditional on August 2, and 413 of the 436 second-round villages on October 7 (conditional means a village needs some supplementary work on land lease talks, funding plans or grid connection, but can proceed within a short time).

That figure became an issue on November 9 at a plenary session of the National Assembly's Special Committee on Budget and Accounts on the 2027 budget bill. Democratic Party lawmaker Lim Ho-seon (Jeungpyeong, Jincheon and Eumseong, North Chungcheong) pointed out that the government plans to raise the number of villages to 2,200 by next year while the budget centres on 28.5 billion won in interest subsidies. Lim said that virtually no village has reached a loan agreement or construction, and warned that residents could be "reduced to bystanders" if outside contractors and consulting brokers step in.

Lim said next year's 15 billion won for agrivoltaics is concentrated on pilot research and building the regulatory framework, and that at least 2,000 farms actually want to take part. Agriculture Minister Song Mi-ryung replied, in effect, that two pilot projects are under way and no extra budget is needed. The third round of applications for Sunlight Income Villages was open until October 30, and the selection results will be announced at the end of December.

Citizen Energy View

In the second round, the Ministry of the Interior and Safety placed 23 villages that fully met the project requirements, including forming a cooperative, securing land and having a funding plan, in a fast-track group. The remaining 413 villages are either still negotiating land leases or need to supplement funding plans or grid connection. In the first round 86 of 129 applicants were selected, and in the second round 436 of 608.
