Shetland substation community fund of £530,000 to be shaped by residents

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.22

A UK transmission company will open a fund for communities around the Mossy Hill wind farm connection substation in summer 2027, with local consultation from December.

A new community fund worth £530,000 will be created for communities around a new wind farm connection substation in Shetland, Scotland. Grid company SSEN Transmission announced on November 19 that it will open the Mossy Hill Local Fund in summer 2027, with its use to be decided based on residents' views.

The fund is linked to a project connecting Statkraft's Mossy Hill Wind Farm on Shetland Mainland to the transmission network. The company will build a new 132kV substation and associated infrastructure for this, and Shetland Islands Council granted planning permission for the substation in June 2025. The area of benefit listed on the company's website is Lerwick, Scalloway and Tingwall, and Whiteness and Weisdale.

The £530,000 matches the per-substation rate in the UK government's guidance on community funds for transmission infrastructure, issued in March 2025. The guidance sets £200,000 for every kilometre of overhead line and applies only to projects that have not started main construction. Funds are paid as a lump sum around the start of construction and must be delivered within 15 years. Even if some money is allocated across a wider area, communities closest to the infrastructure must receive a share. Local government supports engagement but does not take part in decisions (decision-makers must not benefit from funded projects).

The company earlier signed the Shetland Development Charter, led by the EmPowering Shetland partnership, committing to an anticipated £8.9 million of community benefit funding. Construction of the first Shetland HVDC link supported an average of 243 local jobs, and £69 million was spent directly with Shetland businesses. The company plans to invest more than £700 million in its Shetland network programme, including a second link, supporting around 286 local jobs. Engagement on the Mossy Hill fund begins in December with key local stakeholders and continues into early next year, followed by public consultation in 2027.

Citizen Energy View

The Shetland fund starts from a government-set rate of £530,000 per substation, while residents decide how it is spent. Fixing a host community's share by a published formula rather than at the developer's discretion narrows room for disputes over the amount. But a one-off fund is not enough; communities need ownership stakes in power plants that share profits every year for the benefit to last. Korea should also let residents decide how support around transmission lines and substations is used, and look at linking it with community investment.
