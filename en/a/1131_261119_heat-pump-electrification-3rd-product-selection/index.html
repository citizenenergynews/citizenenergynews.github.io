Korea opens third heat-pump product selection for heating electrification, closing 2 December

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.19

The climate ministry is taking applications from manufacturers. Domestic final assembly earns up to 10 points, only products scoring 80 or more are chosen, and they are used in a subsidy scheme worth up to 14 million won per unit.

Korea's Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment is selecting products for the third time for its heating electrification programme, which subsidises household air-source heat pumps. The ministry published its plan for the third round of product selection on 18 November. Manufacturers submit documents by email to the Korea Environmental Industry and Technology Institute, and an expert committee scores them through document review and company presentations, selecting products that score 80 or more out of 100.

The evaluation is split into 70 quantitative points and 30 qualitative points. In-house factory production and the timing of domestic production are each worth 10 points: final assembly at a domestic factory as of the notice date earns 10 points, by the end of 2026 earns 8, and by the end of 2027 earns 6. Performance points require a seasonal coefficient of performance (SCOP) of at least 3.3 at a 55°C flow temperature and 4.5 at 35°C, rated noise of 65dB or less, and a maximum flow temperature of at least 65°C. If a product fails to reach 20 points on performance, all other items are scored zero. Refrigerants must have a global warming potential of 750 or less, and products using propane (R290) refrigerant are excluded on safety grounds for residential installations. Products that keep stock of key parts for at least eight years, or that check a fault on site within two days of a report, earn 10 points for each item.

The selected products will be used to replace LPG and kerosene boilers with heat pumps in detached houses in warmer regions such as Jeju, South Jeolla, South Gyeongsang, Busan, Ulsan and Gwangju. According to the subsidy guidelines, this year's budget is 36.125 billion won (14.45 billion won from the national government), and the scheme covers 40% from national funds and 30% from local funds of up to 14 million won per unit. Priority goes to households that use or plan to install rooftop solar of 3kW or more and to households in areas without city gas. Installed heat pumps are in principle to store heat in a thermal storage tank and run first on electricity from hours when renewable generation is plentiful.

Applications close at 6 p.m. on 2 December, and the results will be published on the websites of the ministry and the Korea Environmental Industry and Technology Institute.

Citizen Energy View

The heating electrification programme subsidises 70% of up to 14 million won per unit and gives priority to homes with rooftop solar. Running a heat pump instead of an oil boiler and storing heat with surplus daytime solar makes rural households' heating bills less exposed to outside fuel prices. Points for domestic assembly and eight years of parts stock also matter for rural households that otherwise wait long for repairs. We hope more local governments plan village solar and heat-pump deployment as a single package.
