Naju runs pre-consulting for 12 villages ahead of 2027 Sunlight Income Village call

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.19

Naju City and the Korea Energy Agency are visiting 12 villages in 7 townships from November 16 to 27 to help them prepare for the 2027 call, with preliminary villages to be picked in December.

Naju, a city in the Jeonnam-Gwangju Integrated Special City, is running door-to-door pre-consulting to choose which villages will apply to the Ministry of the Interior and Safety's 2027 Sunlight Income Village call. The targets are 12 villages in seven townships that signed up, and the visits run from November 16 to 27. Staff from the Korea Energy Agency and city officials visit each village together. On the first day, the 16th, residents heard a briefing at the Dongchon village hall in Dasi township.

The city picked the 12 villages after a village-by-village demand survey, field briefings for village heads and other representatives in each township, and applications. The consulting explains how to obtain residents' consent, how to hold a village general meeting and what is required to set up a cooperative. The city said it is focusing on explaining the project to all residents so that information does not stay with village heads and a few others.

Villages that finish consulting will decide for themselves at a village general meeting whether to take part. Those that go ahead will set up a cooperative and look for a site for the plant before the call. The final list of "preliminary Sunlight Income Villages" is due in December, and the city said it will look at both preparation and each village's commitment.

In a village-level demand survey in February, Naju received expressions of interest from 26 villages totalling 16.9MW. The city offered four facility models: idle land (miscellaneous land and slopes), agrivoltaics (fields, paddies and orchards), rooftops (warehouses and livestock sheds) and public land, at 300kW to 1MW per village. Selected villages can cover up to 85% of facility costs with long-term, low-interest loans from the renewable energy financing program, and pairing an energy storage system (ESS) is being considered where grid capacity is short. About 100 village heads and residents attended a project briefing at city hall on April 17.

Citizen Energy View

Twenty-six villages raised their hands in the survey, but only 12 have reached the consulting stage. Village projects last when most residents, not just a few village heads, understand the structure and risks and agree to them. Naju's approach of sharing information with the whole village should become a standard for other cities and counties preparing for the call. Villages that miss out on preliminary selection should keep getting help with setting up cooperatives and finding sites so they can try again the following year.
