South Chungcheong fire service finishes study on fire-safety grades for battery facilities, weighs risk-based prevention

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.19

Final report meeting on Nov. 18 after a four-month study by Mokwon University's industry-academic cooperation foundation / August fire at a Dangjin battery plant burned about 15 tonnes of lithium-ion batteries

The South Chungcheong Province fire headquarters has finished a study on a "battery facility fire-safety grading system", which rates each battery facility's fire risk and applies different prevention measures by grade, and held a final report meeting in the provincial office's main conference room on Nov. 18. Mokwon University's industry-academic cooperation foundation carried out the study over four months, from July to November.

The researchers surveyed battery facilities in the province on site to identify hazards, assessed a fire-safety grade for each facility and proposed prevention measures to match each grade. Facilities differ in where they are installed and how they are run, so their fire risks differ too, and the study aims to replace the practice of managing them all under a single standard.

On Aug. 31, a fire at battery maker Vitzrocell in Hapdeok-eup, Dangjin, burned about 234 square metres of storage warehouse and about 15 tonnes of lithium-ion batteries. The fire headquarters plans to review how to use the results in policy and to firm up more specific prevention measures for higher-risk facilities, but the grading criteria and start date were not part of the day's announcement.

Citizen Energy View

According to the annex table of the Korea Energy Agency's community solar programme notice issued on Nov. 13, six Sunlight Income Villages on three distribution lines in Gongju, Cheongyang and Buyeo in South Chungcheong, with a combined assessed capacity of 5,565kW, are also eligible for energy storage systems. As storage units go in close to villages, fire safety bears directly on whether residents accept them. If the grading system covers village storage units and not only factories and warehouses, residents will have a basis for judgement. When it sets the grading criteria, the province should include village storage systems and disclose the assessment results to the residents of the villages concerned.
