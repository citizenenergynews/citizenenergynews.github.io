Korea to let low-income households claim gas discounts at actual homes

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.19

Under a trade ministry draft, recipients and near-poor households could apply at the home they actually live in from December 11. The winter cap of up to 148,000 won a month runs to November 2027.

Korea is moving to let basic livelihood security recipients and near-poor households apply for city gas bill discounts at the home they actually live in, not only at their registered address, from December 11. The change is in a draft partial amendment to the Guidelines on Calculating Caps on City Gas Bill Discounts, for which the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources gave administrative notice on November 6. The amendment would take effect on December 1, with only the actual-residence clause starting on December 11.

The new clause applies when the gas wholesaler applies on a recipient's behalf (proxy application). The wholesaler may guide recipients to choose between the address on their resident registration and the actual residence registered in the social security information system. If the chosen residence does not match the one in the system, the wholesaler reapplies using the registered address. At present, applications can only be filed with the city gas company or community service center covering the registered address, among a few other channels.

The current discount cap table (Annex 1) applies only until November 30 under the addendum, and the draft extends it to November 30, 2027. Under the table, livelihood, medical, housing and education benefit recipients and near-poor households that do not receive energy vouchers get a cap of 148,000 won a month for cooking and heating from December to March. The same groups receiving vouchers get 86,000 won a month, while persons with severe disabilities, national merit recipients and independence patriots get 72,000 won. Holders of near-poor certificates and multi-child households get 18,000 won. From April to November, the cooking and heating cap ranges from 2,470 to 9,900 won a month, and discounts apply only to usage charges, excluding the base fee.

The ministry enacted the guidelines on November 28 last year, setting a winter support cap of up to 592,000 won over four months. Comments on the draft can be sent to the Gas Industry Division by mail, email or the e-People online public hearing until November 26.

Citizen Energy View

Vulnerable households whose registered address differs from where they actually live have found it hard to get city gas discounts. Since this amendment opens the actual-residence option only for proxy applications, the same option should be opened for people applying themselves so fewer households fall through the cracks. Rather than extending the cap one year at a time, support for energy self-reliance such as insulation upgrades and rooftop solar should be bundled in so the same worries do not return every winter. Local welfare officials and energy welfare groups should submit comments by November 26.
