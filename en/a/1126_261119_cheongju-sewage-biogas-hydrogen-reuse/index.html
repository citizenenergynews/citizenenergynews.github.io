Cheongju sewage plant turns biogas into 26,801kg of hydrogen in four months, plans 370-tonne-a-day integrated biogas facility

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.19

Clean hydrogen plant running since May lifts monthly output from 2,529kg to 8,316kg / 173.5-billion-won private-investment facility to go underground at the plant; 34,800 tonnes of treated wastewater a day go to industry

According to figures Cheongju City released on Nov. 16 on its sewerage resource-recycling projects, the clean hydrogen plant inside the Cheongju public sewage treatment plant used 277,747 normal cubic metres of biogas to produce 26,801kg of hydrogen over the four months from May to August.

The plant, called "HTWO ENERGY Cheongju", was brought in under an agreement the city signed with Hyundai Motor in December 2024, and uses biogas from household sewage and organic waste to make enough hydrogen to fill about 100 hydrogen cars a day. Monthly output rose from 2,529kg in May to 8,316kg in August.

An integrated biogas facility that will treat 370 tonnes of organic waste a day together — including 232 tonnes of food waste, 93 tonnes of sewage sludge and 45 tonnes of night soil — is a 173.5-billion-won private investment project to be built by 2030. In December 2025 the city signed an implementation agreement with an operator whose lead investor is POSCO E&C, and plans to put the facility underground on a 17,554-square-metre site inside the treatment plant, producing 30,000 cubic metres of biogas a day. A reuse plant run as a build-transfer-operate private investment project for 20 years from September 2024 produces 34,800 tonnes of treated wastewater a day and supplies it to local companies as industrial water, and a second-phase plant is planned for construction and completion in 2029-2031 after a feasibility review.

Citizen Energy View

Making 26,801kg of hydrogen in four months from a public sewage plant's biogas shows that a city's waste is itself an energy source. Yet the hydrogen plant runs under an agreement with Hyundai Motor, and the integrated biogas facility and the reuse plant are private investment projects, and how the income is shared is not in the announcement. If the energy comes from the water and food residents use and throw away, residents should be able to see where the money goes too. Cheongju should disclose the income-sharing terms of these agreements and hydrogen sales figures, and consider a way for residents to take part in deciding how the biogas from the integrated facility, to be built by 2030, is used and who earns from it.
