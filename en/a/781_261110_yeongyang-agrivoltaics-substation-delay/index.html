Agrivoltaics Law Takes Effect December 17, but Yeongyang Substation Power Supply Slips by Two and a Half Years

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.10

The 154kV Yeongyang substation moves from June 2027 to the end of December 2029; a roughly 29km transmission line and about 76 pylons have not yet started

Power supply from the 154kV Yeongyang substation in Yeongyang County, North Gyeongsang Province, has slipped by two and a half years, from the original June 2027 to the end of December 2029. According to Korea Electric Power Corporation disclosure data, the substation will stand in the Ori area of Wolil-myeon, and final completion is set for June 2030. A roughly 29km section of 154kV transmission line from Socheon-myeon in Bonghwa County to Yeongyang County, along with about 76 pylons, has not yet started construction.

On November 6, the Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs held the Agrivoltaics Coexistence Forum in Cheongju, North Chungcheong Province, attended by more than 220 farmers and local government officials. At the event, the ministry said facilities must keep shading below 30 percent (the share of area that blocks sunlight), structures at least 2.5m high and posts 4m apart. A regulatory impact analysis the ministry wrote on September 11 factored in grid-connection conditions (linking generation equipment to the power grid) when estimating the number of permits over the three years after enforcement. The Act on the Promotion of and Support for Agrivoltaic Power Generation is scheduled to take effect on December 17.
