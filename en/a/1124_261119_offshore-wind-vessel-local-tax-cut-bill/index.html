Korean bill would cut acquisition tax by 85% on offshore wind installation vessels

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.19

An amendment to the Restriction of Special Local Taxation Act filed by 10 lawmakers would also halve property tax for five years; it would take effect in January 2027, with comments due Nov. 22.

An amendment to Korea's Restriction of Special Local Taxation Act has been submitted to the National Assembly that would cut acquisition tax by 85% on vessels bought to build and maintain offshore wind power facilities. Ten lawmakers filed it on Nov. 12, and it falls under the Public Administration and Security Committee.

The amendment adds a new Paragraph 5 to Article 64 (special taxation to support shipping and ports). It covers vessels acquired for the construction and after-care of offshore wind facilities under Article 2, Item 2 of the Special Act on Promotion of Offshore Wind Power and Industry Development, as specified by presidential decree (which vessels qualify has not yet been decided). The acquisition tax cut would apply until Dec. 31, 2029. Vessels in that use on the tax base date would have property tax cut by 50% for five years from the date the tax liability first arises.

If a vessel is not put to that use within one year of acquisition without a valid reason, the reduced acquisition tax would be clawed back. The same applies if it is sold, donated or put to other use before being used directly for two years. The current Article 64 reduces acquisition and property tax for international vessels, coastal passenger and cargo ships, ocean-going ships and eco-friendly vessels, but wind turbine installation vessels (WTIVs) are not covered. The sponsors said the aim is to secure dedicated vessels for transporting, installing and maintaining large equipment in time and to strengthen the domestic shipbuilding industry.

The law would take effect on Jan. 1, 2027, and apply to vessels acquired after that. The National Assembly notice period runs from Nov. 13 to 22, and comments can be submitted on the Assembly's legislative notice website.

Citizen Energy View

This tax cut goes to the companies that buy offshore wind installation vessels. Acquisition and property taxes are also part of the budgets of coastal local governments. If taxes are cut to speed up offshore wind, there should also be arrangements to return part of the profits from those wind farms to fishers and coastal residents. We hope the Public Administration and Security Committee will examine local benefit-sharing alongside the scope of the presidential decree that will define eligible vessels.
