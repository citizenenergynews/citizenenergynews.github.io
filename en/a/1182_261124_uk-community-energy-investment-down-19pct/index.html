UK community energy investment falls 19%, 359MW stalled

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.24

The 2026 state of the sector report by community energy bodies in England, Scotland and Wales. Some 89,023 members of 660 organisations run 452MW of generation.

Investment in the UK's citizen and community energy organisations fell by 19% in a year. That is a finding of the "Community Energy State of the Sector 2026" report, published in November by Community Energy England together with its counterparts in Scotland and Wales. The 660 organisations across the UK (up about 7% on last year) have 89,023 members and 4,683 volunteers. They run more than 1,600 operational sites, 96% of which are fully community-owned.

Their generation capacity stands at 452MW, up 39% since 2020, producing an estimated 661GWh a year (equivalent to about 244,000 homes). Revenue from generation for 2025 is estimated at around £70 million, and £6.3 million of community benefit funding was spent locally in the same year. Of the organisations' spending, 73% stayed in the local area.

Projects totalling 359MW at 46 organisations have stalled, locking up £29 million a year in potential revenue. The organisations named staff funding, access to the grid and the lack of a viable business model, in that order, as the top barriers to growth, and net employment across the sector fell over the year. New projects sell electricity at about 7p per kWh, far below the roughly 15p earned by projects from the feed-in tariff (FIT) era. For 45% of operational generation, those tariff contracts expire from the 2030s.

The report made 11 policy demands of government, including a minimum floor price for community electricity sales, public bodies buying electricity from local community energy groups, and priority access to public land and rooftops. Of the organisations with stalled projects, 70% are still developing, and many have switched from ground-mounted to rooftop solar to avoid planning barriers. Projects in development total 462MW (257MW wind and 201MW solar) and need a further £27.6 million to be delivered.

Citizen Energy View

Even UK community energy, with nearly 90,000 members, has 359MW on hold in the face of a 7p per kWh sale price and a blocked grid. That residents' power plants are held back by sale prices and grid access is equally true of Korea's solar income villages, many selected on grid and funding conditions. If 73% of spending stays local, then every unit of that electricity bought by public bodies keeps money in the area. Korea's local governments and public companies should start by giving residents' cooperatives first access to public rooftops and committing to buy their electricity.
