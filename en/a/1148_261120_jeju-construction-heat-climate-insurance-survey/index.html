Jeju heat climate insurance pays little, survey runs to 5 December

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.20

The scheme pays day labourers on public building sites 17,210 won an hour when heat stops work. Jeju will use the survey to revise payout conditions for 2027.

Jeju Special Self-Governing Province is running a survey until 5 December to revise the payout conditions of its "climate insurance", which makes up lost income for construction day labourers who stop work in heat waves. The province said on 19 November that payouts have been low because sites find it hard to stop work under pressure from construction deadlines and schedule delays.

The insurance, piloted on 28 July as the first in Korea, covers day labourers enrolled in the retirement mutual-aid scheme at publicly ordered construction sites worth 100 million won or more. At present a payout requires all conditions to be met: a heat wave warning, four hours of work in the morning and a work stoppage after 1 p.m. It is "index-based insurance" (paying out when a preset indicator is crossed), so workers need not prove losses separately as long as the Korea Meteorological Administration warning and the work stoppage match. Payouts are 17,210 won an hour, 80% of the 172,068 won daily wage for ordinary labourers converted to an hourly rate, for up to four hours.

Funding totals 1 billion won over three years: 900 million won from the insurance associations' win-win fund, which the province won in the Financial Services Commission's "win-win insurance" contest in March, plus 100 million won from the provincial budget. Jeju's heat wave advisory days rose from 38 in 2023 to 80 in 2025, and there were 107 heat-related illness patients in 2025.

The survey began on the 16th and is run by the Jeju Economic and Trade Promotion Agency. Anyone can take part in the residents' survey, answering questions on awareness of the insurance and expanding who it covers. The site survey asks site managers, safety managers and workers at province-ordered sites about actual work stoppages and whether the payout conditions are appropriate. The province's director general for climate and energy said that for the insurance to take root as a system that protects workers' safety and livelihoods in heat waves, "the voice of the sites matters". The province will consult the insurance industry and others and reflect the results in payout conditions and coverage for 2027. The Act on Climate Crisis Adaptation and Resilience takes effect on 23 September 2027.

Citizen Energy View

In Jeju, where heat wave advisory days more than doubled in two years, a system in which the public sector supports the income of workers who stop work has appeared for the first time. But if sites racing against deadlines cannot stop work and almost no payouts are made, it becomes a safety net in name only. To avoid pushing the cost of climate adaptation onto individual workers, payout conditions should fit real sites, and the climate safety net should be widened with funds residents build together, like profits from locally generated renewable energy. We hope many site workers and residents take part in the survey, open until 5 December.
