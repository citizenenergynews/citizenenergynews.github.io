Korea's Third ESS Tender Picks 14 Sites, 1,194MW, Double the Second Round

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.27

Six sites in South Jeolla, five in North Jeolla, one in South Chungcheong and two in Jeju are preferred bidders. Final volumes will be confirmed on December 4, and facilities must be built by February 2029.

In the third tender of Korea's central contract market for energy storage systems (ESS), 14 sites totaling 1,194MW in South Jeolla, North Jeolla, South Chungcheong and Jeju were selected as preferred bidders. The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment and the Korea Power Exchange (KPX) announced the results on November 26 and said final winning volumes would be announced on December 4. That is more than double the 565MW (six sites in South Jeolla and one in Jeju) chosen as preferred bidders in the second round announced on February 12.

By region, South Jeolla has six sites with 539MW, North Jeolla five sites with 480MW, Nonsan in South Chungcheong one site with 96MW, and Pyoseon in Jeju two sites with 79MW. The South Jeolla sites connect to five substations including Haenam, and the North Jeolla sites to three substations including Soseong. Winning bids on the mainland reached 1,115MW, above the announced volume (awards of up to 105%, or 1,155MW, were allowed), while Jeju took 79MW of its 80MW.

In KPX Notice No. 2026-05 of September 22, the mainland volume was 1,100MW and 6,600MWh, with six hours of continuous discharge required. The trading period is 15 years, during which battery warranted life must stay at 70% or more and operating efficiency at 65% or more. Bids were accepted in 1MW units between more than 10MW and less than 100MW, and when facilities are decommissioned after the contract ends, spent batteries must be disposed of in Korea. Scores were split 50 points for price and 50 for non-price factors (a ratio adopted in the second round, replacing the earlier 60:40). The ministry said it broadened site evaluation from a focus on grid-managed substations to an analysis of grid conditions nationwide.

Winning bidders must obtain power generation business licenses by August 2027 and complete construction by February 2029 (the second round's deadline was December 2027). The notice states that a winning bid is cancelled if the license is not obtained within nine months of the announcement of winners. The ministry and KPX plan to open the fourth central contract market in the first half of 2027.

Citizen Energy View

Of the 1,194MW, South Jeolla's 539MW and North Jeolla's 480MW make up the Honam share. That volume still concentrated in Honam even after site evaluation was widened to grid conditions nationwide shows how urgent the region's grid situation is. Yet bids must exceed 10MW, so village and cooperative power plants cannot enter this market. Whether the 10MW floor remains in the fourth-round notice in the first half of 2027 is the first thing small operators will need to check.
