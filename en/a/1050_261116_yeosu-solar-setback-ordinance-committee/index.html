Yeosu Committee Passes Ordinance Cutting Solar Setback From Homes From 300 to 200 Meters

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.16

Road setback limit of 100 meters is dropped and wind standards stay; matching the 200-meter cap in the enforcement decree, final vote set for Nov. 17

The Ocean City Construction Committee of the Yeosu City Council on Nov. 11 passed, as submitted, the amendment to the Yeosu Urban Planning Ordinance that cuts the required distance between solar plants and homes from 300 meters to 200 meters. The bill, sponsored by council member Gu Min-ho, also scraps the 100-meter setback from roads. The distance does not apply where fewer than five homes are nearby, and even where there are five or more, a plant can go ahead if every household head consents (public policy projects and industrial complexes are excluded). For wind power, the current standards of 1,500 meters from homes and 300 meters from roads stay.

The basis is the Enforcement Decree of the Act on the Development, Use and Dissemination of Renewable Energy. Promulgated as Presidential Decree No. 36586 on Aug. 18 and in force since Sept. 18, Article 27-3 of the decree sets the range that ordinances may use at up to 200 meters from homes for solar and up to 1,500 meters from homes and 500 meters from roads for wind. Yeosu set its solar setback from homes at that 200-meter cap.

During review, council member Song Ha-jin proposed lowering the figures further, to 100 meters from homes for solar and 1,000 meters from homes and 200 meters from roads for wind. Yoon Dae-geun, head of the city's urban planning division, said complaints from residents over installations are frequent, so it is better to enforce the bill as it stands first and ease it in stages if needed, and the committee passed the original bill without changes. The city council was scheduled to give final approval at the second plenary session of its 259th regular session on Nov. 17.
