Colorado Plug-In Solar Nonprofit Launches as State Law Bars Utilities From Requiring Approval Before Installation

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.11

Law covers devices of up to 1,920 watts; limits on unreasonable bans apply from 1 January 2027

Solar Access Colorado, a nonprofit in the US state of Colorado, has launched, local media reported on 10 November. The Denver-based group is aiming at renters and apartment residents who cannot easily use a roof, and has opened preorders for plug-in (small panels that plug into an outlet) solar kits.

The state law it relies on is HB26-1007 (improving a customer's ability to use distributed energy resources), which the governor signed on 7 May. The law covers one device per address, with output to the grid of up to 1,920 watts, that is labeled and listed by a nationally recognized testing laboratory. Utilities cannot require customers who use such a device to get approval before installing it or to pay a fee. A homeowners' association (HOA) covenant that unreasonably blocks a device is unenforceable, while owners may be required to secure the device firmly. The limits on unreasonable bans apply from 1 January 2027, and the Public Utilities Commission must revise its interconnection rules for meter collar adapters by 31 December 2026.
