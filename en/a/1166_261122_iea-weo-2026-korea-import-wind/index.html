IEA World Energy Outlook sees Korean wind power at 130TWh or more by 2040

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.22

The International Energy Agency put Korea's energy import dependence at about 80%, the highest in the world alongside Japan. It named offshore wind, transmission grids and energy storage as the tasks for Korea's 100GW renewables plan.

The International Energy Agency's World Energy Outlook 2026, released on 19 November, projects that South Korea's wind power generation will grow to more than 130TWh by 2040. Under the Stated Policies Scenario (a pathway reflecting policies countries have officially announced), wind output rises from less than 5TWh to that level. Korea's Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment issued a press reference note on 20 November summarising the report's analysis of Korea.

Over the same period, nuclear generation rises from 185TWh to more than 230TWh and gas-fired generation falls from 175TWh to less than 120TWh. The IEA said Korea could cut both import dependence and carbon emissions if current plans, including 100GW of renewables by 2030, are carried out. It named faster offshore wind deployment, transmission grid expansion, reform of grid operation, and more energy storage systems (ESS) and pumped hydro as the tasks. When the ministry announced a grid innovation plan on 30 September to absorb 100GW, it said new projects in the Honam region were blocked from grid connection until 2033.

The report named Korea, whose net imports cover about 80% of energy demand, alongside Japan as the countries most dependent on energy imports. The IEA described this year's Strait of Hormuz disruption, which cut supply of 2.8 billion barrels of oil and 60 billion cubic metres of gas, as the largest supply shock ever. Korea released oil stocks, temporarily capped wholesale prices of oil products and spent about 7 billion dollars on consumer support. The IEA cited Korea's expansion of heat pumps and its higher renewable targets as examples of crisis-response transition policy.

Globally, solar power accounted for the largest share of the increase in electricity generation in 2025. Even so, the IEA calculates that global temperatures would rise by about 2.9°C by 2100 under current policies alone, and by 2.5°C even with announced policies. The report proposed using the four pillars of the Net Zero scenario as a benchmark in the second Global Stocktake (GST), which begins at the end of 2026.

Citizen Energy View

When Korea imports about 80% of its energy, a shock like Hormuz quickly becomes a burden on households. Wind power of 130TWh and 100GW of renewables are targets on paper, and it is local communities where projects stall while grids are delayed. Power plants owned by villages and cooperatives return profits to residents and widen local consent for new plants and transmission lines. Resident equity participation should be built into offshore wind and grid plans from the start.
