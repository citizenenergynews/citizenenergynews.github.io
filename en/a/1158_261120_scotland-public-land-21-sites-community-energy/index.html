Scotland opens 21 public sites to community renewables

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.20

Hospital, court car park and business park land is on the list. A day later the government announced £991,492 for 25 community projects, from churches to island communities.

The Scottish Government has put forward 21 public sites, including hospital, court and business park land and buildings, as potential locations for community renewable energy projects. The government published the list on November 18, and the next day announced £991,492 in support for 25 community projects.

The sites are land, buildings and rooftops identified by public bodies and local authorities as part of the government's first 100 days programme. Of the 21, 13 belong to Highlands and Islands Enterprise, and three court sites and three council sites are also included. Eleven are earmarked for solar, one for battery storage and eight have not yet been assessed, and the solar sites include four car parks such as Glasgow Sheriff Court. Local Energy Scotland, which published the list, said it has not assessed the sites' suitability, so community groups must carry out their own feasibility studies. The government also updated guidance for the Community Empowerment Act 2015 to set out how public bodies can help community projects (the act gives community bodies the right to request the transfer of public land and buildings).

The projects funded on the 19th include solar at churches, sports facilities, charities and public buildings, as well as island wind schemes. The largest award was £167,735 for rooftop solar at King's Church in Aberdeen. In Barra and Vatersay, an Energy Local Club will let residents benefit more directly from electricity generated by community-owned wind power. Including these awards, the fund has supported 66 community energy projects this year with around £4 million.

The government's Community and Renewable Energy Scheme (CARES) has so far provided more than £83 million, supporting over 1,200 projects and 75MW of renewable energy. The government expects rooftop solar on three schools in Wallacetown, South Ayrshire, to generate around £1 million for community investment over 25 years. Applications for a new capital round of the Community Energy Growth Fund, financed by the UK government's publicly owned company Great British Energy, have been open since the 19th.

Citizen Energy View

Scotland has gathered 21 sites offered by public bodies into a single list that community groups can look through. The government's estimate that solar on three school roofs will leave around £1 million in a village over 25 years shows that public roofs can underpin community income. Because the list has not been checked for suitability, support for feasibility studies must go with it if the sites are to become real power plants. Our local governments should start by listing public car parks, government building roofs and idle public land and sharing it first with residents' cooperatives.
