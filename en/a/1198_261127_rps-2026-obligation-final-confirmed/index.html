Korea Finalizes 2026 RPS Obligation, 156,018MWh Below January Notice

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.27

The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment set the 29 obligated suppliers' total at 69,174,827MWh, or 94,077,763 RECs, on November 24. Korea Southern Power saw the biggest cut.

The 2026 obligation that 29 suppliers must meet under Korea's Renewable Portfolio Standard (RPS) has been finalized at 69,174,827MWh. In Notice No. 2026-1055 issued on November 24, the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment set the obligation at 94,077,763 renewable energy certificates (RECs). That is 156,018MWh, or 212,185 RECs, less than the 69,330,845MWh in the provisional notice (No. 2026-77) issued on January 30.

The January notice said a final notice would follow once the Korea Electric Power Statistics approved by Statistics Korea were finalized, and this notice provides those final figures. Obligations changed for 11 of the 29 suppliers and stayed the same for 18. Korea Southern Power had the largest cut at 68,823MWh. Korea South-East Power (57,828MWh), Korea Midland Power (41,521MWh) and GS EPS (35,406MWh) followed. Korea East-West Power's obligation rose by 31,336MWh and Korea Western Power's by 17,388MWh.

Group I, made up of Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power (KHNP) and five state-owned generation companies, carries 50,151,101MWh, or 72.5% of the total. KHNP alone has the largest share at 13,210,638MWh. The REC obligation is calculated by multiplying MWh by a conversion ratio of 1.36 (Article 37(1) of the Rules on Issuing Certificates and Operating the Trading Market).

According to the ministry's November 23 press release, the amended Act on the Promotion of the Development, Use and Diffusion of Renewable Energy, which replaces the RPS with a competitive-bidding contract market, takes effect on January 1, 2027. The current obligation system will be phased out, and the spot market for trading RECs will stay open only until December 31, 2029, three years after the law takes effect. The ministry scheduled the release of draft rules for the contract market and draft amendments to the RPS operating guidelines at a briefing session on November 27.

Citizen Energy View

KHNP and five state-owned generators carry 72.5% of this year's obligation of more than 94.07 million RECs. The money they spend buying RECs ultimately comes back through electricity bills, so it stays in local communities only if it flows to power plants owned by residents and cooperatives. The REC spot market stays open until the end of 2029 even after the shift to a contract market, giving citizen power plants already in operation three more years to sell RECs. Whether small and community-owned facilities then get a separate share in the contract market will become clear in the draft rules the ministry planned to present at its November 27 briefing and in the administrative notice that follows.
