Bill Would Require Renewable Energy on Factory Roofs, With Fines Up to 30 Million Won

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.26

Filed on November 19 by Rep. Park Ji-hye and 10 other lawmakers, it would require equipment when factories and knowledge industry centers above a set size are built or expanded, taking effect one year after promulgation.

A bill amending the Renewable Energy Development, Use and Promotion Act to require renewable energy equipment on roofs when factories are newly built or expanded has been introduced in the National Assembly. The bill (bill No. 2222967), filed by Rep. Park Ji-hye and 10 other lawmakers on November 19, was referred to the Climate, Energy, Environment and Labor Committee on the 20th.

The new Article 12-21 places this obligation on factory owners and others planning new construction, extensions or reconstruction. It covers factories and knowledge industry centers (multi-tenant factory buildings) above a size set by presidential decree, both as defined in the Industrial Cluster Development and Factory Establishment Act. It applies to those seeking approval or amended approval to establish a factory, and to those filing a factory completion report or an amended registration. Those seeking approval to establish a knowledge industry center or signing or amending a tenancy contract under that act are also included. The central and local governments may provide financial and administrative support to those installing the equipment.

If the obligation is not met, mayors, county heads and district heads may order corrections within a set period. If an owner fails to comply within that period without good reason, a non-compliance charge of up to 30 million won may be imposed. Amounts by type and degree of violation are left to presidential decree, and the law would take effect one year after promulgation.

The reasons for the proposal note that the government named industrial complexes and factory roofs as one of four priority policy locations in its plan to deploy 100GW of solar power by 2030. They explain that such roofs need no separate site development, have good grid conditions and public acceptance, and make it easier to cut companies' electricity bills. The EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism and global companies' RE100 requirements are also cited. A day earlier, on November 18, Rep. Park also filed an amendment extending the initial lease period for state and public property used for renewable energy projects to up to 20 years.

Citizen Energy View

Industrial complexes and factory roofs are places where solar power can grow without clearing new land, so a debate on mandatory installation is welcome. But if the obligation is seen only as a cost to factory owners, it may end with the bare minimum capacity. Sunlight on these roofs becomes local income only if arrangements in which factories simply lease their roofs and cooperatives funded by local residents and workers build the plants are also recognized as fulfilling the obligation. That path should be opened when the presidential decree sets the target size and methods of compliance.
