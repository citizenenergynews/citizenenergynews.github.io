Only 5 of Jeonnam-Gwangju's 126 Sunlight Income Villages Can Connect to the Grid Now

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.26

Next year's budget of 7.32 billion won will support 100 villages. At a November 24 budget review, council member Shin Min-ho called for profit-sharing rules to stop gains from going only to investors.

Of the 126 villages selected by the Jeonnam-Gwangju Integrated Special City as Sunlight Income Villages, only five can connect to the power grid right away. City officials gave this answer on November 24 during the special city council's review of the 2027 budget for the Energy Industry Headquarters. Thirty-six villages have been identified as possibly able to connect using energy storage systems (ESS). Officials said they would not release subsidies in advance to villages whose connection dates have not been confirmed.

Next year's budget for supporting Sunlight Income Village power facilities is 7.32 billion won for 100 villages. The program covers part of the residents' own share of the cost of building the facilities. The officials' profit analysis included an example in which a village raises 136.5 million won as a common fund. The officials explained that the "common benefit worth 140,000 won a month" in the analysis is not money paid directly to each household but assumes shared use.

Council member Shin Min-ho (Suncheon 6, Democratic Party) called for concrete profit-sharing rules and implementation plans so that power generation profits do not go only to some investors. Shin said the city had not fully grasped the state of common funds in the selected villages and asked for financing alternatives and rules for returning profits to residents who do not invest. He also asked the city to explain the assumptions behind using an installation cost of 1.5 billion won per 1MW in the budget calculation but 1.3 billion won in the profit analysis. For the agrivoltaic ESS installation support project, which has 900 million won from the special city budget, he urged the city to settle who pays the rest of the cost, who owns the equipment and who receives operating revenue.

"It should not be money that is gone after one meal, but lasting income that residents can feel in their daily lives," Shin said. The Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs on November 25 pre-announced a draft notice capping farmers' agrivoltaic projects at 200kW per project, and the underlying law takes effect on December 17.

Citizen Energy View

If only five of the 126 selected villages can send power right away, the income promised to residents is tied to the grid construction schedule. Grid connection schedules and responsibility for running ESS must be set village by village first so that subsidies are not wasted. If profits flow to a few households that can afford to invest, sunlight income becomes private investment rather than a village project. Writing the share for non-investing residents and the use of common funds into the cooperative's articles should be a condition for support.
