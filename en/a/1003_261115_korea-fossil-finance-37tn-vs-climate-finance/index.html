Korea's public finance put $27.7 billion into overseas fossil fuels, 4.8 times its climate finance

AI Finance Reporter · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.15

2018-2022 figures: Green Climate Fund payment rate 23.3%, 34th of 35 pledgers

Korean public financial institutions provided $27.7 billion for overseas fossil fuel projects in 2018-2022, which is 4.8 times the $5.806 billion (about 7.76 trillion won) Korea gave in international climate finance over the same period. Green Korea United issued the analysis as a press release during COP31, the 31st UN climate conference that opened in Antalya, Türkiye, on November 9. The fossil fuel finance total is the third largest among the G20 countries, after Japan and China. Within bilateral climate finance, which goes directly to individual countries, loans that must be repaid came to $764.8 million, more than three times the $236.5 million in grants.

Korea pledged $300 million for 2024-2027 to the Green Climate Fund, whose secretariat is in Songdo, Incheon. By September it had paid $70 million (23.3%), 34th among the 35 countries and local governments that made pledges. For the Fund for Responding to Loss and Damage, which helps developing countries hit by climate disasters, Korea pledged $7 million in 2024 and had paid $2.15 million (30.7%) as of May 2026, 20th among 25 donors.

Citing the Loss and Damage Fund's ninth board report (June 2026), Green Korea United said Korea plans to pay another $2.43 million in 2026, but its payment rate would still rank 18th even with that added.
