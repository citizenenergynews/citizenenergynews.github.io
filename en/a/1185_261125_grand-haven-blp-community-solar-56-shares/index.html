Grand Haven's Public Solar Garden Is 56 Shares Short of Construction

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.25

A one-time $570 share earns bill credits for one panel's output for 20 years. Construction starts once 333 shares are sold; the deadline is December 15.

A 300 kW community solar project planned by the Grand Haven Board of Light & Power (BLP), the public utility of Grand Haven, Michigan, is 56 shares short of the level needed to start construction. Local station WGHN reported on November 23 that 277 shares had been sold so far and that 56 more were needed by December 15. The threshold is 333 shares, or 33% of total capacity.

Each share is 300 watts, the equivalent of one panel, and costs a one-time payment of $570. Participants receive a monthly credit on their electric bill equal to their share of the output multiplied by $0.06385 per kWh. The developer estimates that each share will produce about 400 kWh a year on average. The lease runs for 20 years from the first billing month after the array starts delivering power, and the credit rate stays fixed for that period. Payments are held in an escrow account at Huntington Bank until subscriptions reach 33%.

Any residential, commercial or industrial customer in the City of Grand Haven, the City of Ferrysburg and the townships of Grand Haven, Spring Lake and Robinson can apply, and renters can take part. The array will be built on vacant land next to a power line corridor, on which the city has granted an easement. Credited output cannot exceed the participant's average consumption over the previous 12 months, and the renewable energy certificates (RECs) stay with the BLP and are retired every year. The BLP handles maintenance and repairs, and a participant who moves out of the service area can pass the remaining credits to the new owner or tenant of the home or donate them to a local nonprofit.

The BLP serves more than 15,000 customers, and its investments in solar, wind and landfill gas projects across Michigan make up about 30% of its annual power purchases. If 333 shares are sold, the array will be completed by the end of 2027; if not by December 15, it will not be built and applicants will be fully refunded.

Citizen Energy View

In Grand Haven, a locally owned public utility is building an array on city land and letting renters without roofs join one panel at a time. Building only if 333 shares are sold, and refunding everyone otherwise, keeps residents from carrying much of the risk. Korea's solar programs for public car parks and public land, and its sunlight income villages, could likewise widen the path for residents to join in small units on land provided by local governments. When local governments plan solar on public land, they should design a share for renters and shopkeepers from the start.
