Korea's climate ministry and IEA unveil Asian energy security initiative at COP31; IEA sees room to lift Southeast Asia's electrification rate from 25% to 37%

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.19

Korea joined the pledge to raise electricity to 35% of final energy by 2035 on 11 November / Three areas: crisis response, clean electrification and policy dialogue, with no date yet for an implementation plan

Korea's Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment set up a side event with the International Energy Agency (IEA) at 1 p.m. local time on 18 November (7 p.m. in Korea) at the IEA pavilion of the 31st UN Climate Change Conference (COP31) in Antalya, Türkiye, to present RISE ASIA, an Asian energy security cooperation initiative. The initiative aims to carry the "35 by 35" pledge, which seeks to raise electricity's share of global final energy consumption to 35% by 2035, into cooperation across Asia, and Korea signed up to the pledge on 11 November.

The initiative grew out of a memorandum of understanding the ministry signed with the IEA after President Lee Jae-myung met IEA Executive Director Fatih Birol in September, and it rests on three pillars: sharing energy risk information and responding to crises, clean electrification, and open and inclusive energy policy dialogue. The one-hour programme included a keynote by an IEA senior expert, a presentation by an ASEAN minister and a panel discussion, with the Philippine energy undersecretary, assistant secretaries from Australia and Vietnam, and Song Jae-do, president of the Korea Energy Economics Institute, on the list of attendees.

The IEA planned to present its analysis there that Southeast Asia's electrification rate is about 25% today and could rise to about 37% with existing technology alone. The ministry said it would draw up a detailed implementation plan through joint research with the IEA, but did not say when it would be released.

Citizen Energy View

Raising electricity to 35% of final energy means generating a great deal of new power, and the ministry itself wrote that clean power supply and grids must expand together. Who generates the extra electricity, and where the revenue goes, decides whether electrification becomes the share of large operators or income for local communities. The ministry said it would use RISE ASIA to widen cooperation on technology, infrastructure and finance. The implementation plan should make sure the same finance channels also reach renewable power plants owned by residents and cooperatives at home.
