Yongin council presses city on returns from citizen solar fund

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.20

In an audit on the 18th, Yongin Special City Council's economy and environment committee raised voluntary returns of solar permits, investment losses for older people, a halted energy self-reliant village project and mandatory parking-lot installations.

The economy and environment committee of the Yongin Special City Council in South Korea has asked the city to review how residents take part in the "Citizen Sunlight Fund Park" project and how its power income is returned to them. The committee's audit was held on the 18th, covering the three district offices of Cheoin, Giheung and Suji and seven departments under the Future City Planning Bureau, and the committee made its findings public on the 19th. The project will build 1MW at four public facilities in Yangji-eup; it was selected as a 2026 Gyeonggi RE100 pilot project and plans to supply its power to local companies pursuing RE100 through power purchase agreements (PPAs).

Councilor Lee Ju-yeon asked that the project's benefits actually reach local residents and that the Haetbit (sunlight) Income Village application be pursued in step with related departments. The audit also heard a call to examine why solar power business permits are being voluntarily returned and the concern over investment losses for older people, and to prepare measures to prevent harm. Councilor Kim Jong-eok asked the city to check every renewable energy installation at public buildings and public parking lots and manage them in one place. The councilor also asked that the convergence support program be screened in advance so its budget is not left unspent, and that installations concentrated in Cheoin-gu be extended to apartment complexes and other urban residential areas.

Councilor Park Hee-jeong called for a way to revive the halted energy self-reliant village project and for city-funded projects of its own, and said the mandatory renewable energy installation at public parking lots should proceed through dialogue with cooperatives. The councilor also asked the city to examine whether an RE100 industrial complex could be built to help exporters meet RE100 demands. Councilor Kang Young-woong said electricity demand will grow as electric vehicles spread and semiconductor industrial complexes are built, and asked departments to link their policies and plan to raise self-sufficiency through renewable energy.

According to a report released on the 3rd by the Yongin Research Institute, 17 of the 51 public parking lots the city reviewed have submitted solar installation plans. The Yongin Citizen Sunlight Power Plant (907kW) on idle public land in Baegam-myeon has operated since 2025, combining investment from a citizen cooperative with dividends from power income, and citizen-participation plants No. 2 to 5 are under construction at Giheung Respia, Maseong IC, Yongin Citizens' Sports Center and the Yongin rowing course.

Citizen Energy View

Yongin already has a 907kW citizen sunlight plant and four citizen-participation plants under construction, so the path for residents to invest and receive dividends is open. The concern that older people could lose money as solar permits are returned shows the risk of individuals carrying power investments alone. Investing together through cooperatives and using public land spreads that risk and keeps the income in the region. As the audit asked, the city should publish the fund park's dividend rules for residents and its plan to restart the energy self-reliant village project so citizens can check them.
