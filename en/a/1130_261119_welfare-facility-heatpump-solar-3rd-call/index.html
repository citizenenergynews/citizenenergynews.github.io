Korea funds 80% of heat pumps and solar for welfare facilities

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.19

Private welfare facilities in areas without city gas are eligible. The Korea Energy Agency takes applications on November 23-27, with a budget of 280.6 million won and a cap of 34.8 million won per facility.

Private social welfare facilities that heat with kerosene, LPG or electricity in areas without city gas can get up to 80% of installation costs covered if they install an air-source heat pump together with solar panels. The Korea Energy Agency issued the third call for its 2026 Social Welfare Facility Electrification Project on November 18 and will take applications from 10 a.m. on November 23 to 5 p.m. on November 27. The budget for this round is 280.6 million won, with a cap of 34.8 million won per facility.

Support covers only a package that combines a heat pump and solar, and facilities cannot install just one of the two. The maximum support is 20.27 million won for the heat pump (based on 32kW) and 14.53 million won for solar (based on 12kW). The solar system must be for self-consumption to offset the extra power the heat pump uses, and only low-carbon modules verified at 655kg of carbon emissions or less per kW may be used. Facilities set up or run by the national or local governments are excluded, and only private facilities reported to local governments under Article 34 of the Social Welfare Services Act can apply.

A facility cannot apply alone; it must form a consortium with an energy service company (ESCO or similar) and a company registered for the solar deployment program, and submit a business plan on a dedicated website (min24.energy.or.kr/welfare). Facilities burning fossil fuels such as kerosene or LPG come first in the selection order, followed by facilities with electric boilers and then those with wood pellet or firewood boilers. Evaluation consists of 90 non-quantitative points, including cost appropriateness and savings, and 10 points for boiler age, and facilities scoring 70 or more are chosen in order of score.

The agency will pick recipients in December after document review, evaluation and site checks, and selected consortiums must finish installation within 60 days of signing a performance pledge. Subsidies will be paid from February 2027 after installation is confirmed, and facilities must keep the equipment for three years.

Citizen Energy View

Welfare facilities in areas that city gas does not reach get through winter on kerosene and LPG. Pairing heat pumps with rooftop solar so that facilities produce the electricity they use is a way to cut both heating bills and fossil fuel use. Yet the third-round budget of 280.6 million won would not cover even 10 facilities at the per-facility cap. Local welfare officials and community energy cooperatives should find eligible facilities and help them form consortiums before the November 27 deadline, and the government should raise next year's budget to open this path to more facilities.
