Gas pipes for Yangpyeong villages may ride on Route 88 roadworks

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.20

Gyeonggi councillors, the province and gas supplier Kowon Energy Service met on 19 November over laying a main along a 16.8 km road project. A new supply station would cost about KRW 100 billion.

Laying a city-gas main alongside roadworks on provincial road 88 was discussed as a way to reach Gangha-myeon and Gangsang-myeon in Yangpyeong County, Gyeonggi Province, which have no city-gas supply. Lee Hye-won, vice-chair of the Gyeonggi Provincial Council's Urban Environment Committee (People Power Party, Yangpyeong 2), and four other councillors from Gwangju and Yangpyeong attended the pipeline meeting at the Gyeonggi provincial office on 19 November. Officials from the province's road construction and energy management divisions and from gas company Kowon Energy Service also took part.

The road project covers 16.8 km: a 10.4 km two-lane upgrade from Toechon in Gwangju to Gangha, and a 6.4 km four-lane widening from Gangha to Gangsang. Participants went through the design, compensation and construction schedule for each section and looked at when pipes could be laid, village demand, routes and supply pressure. Of two options put forward by Kowon Energy Service, building a new Korea Gas Corporation supply station in Namhansanseong-myeon, Gwangju, would cost about KRW 100 billion, and the consultations alone usually take six to seven years. Attaching a pipe to the Yanggeun Bridge in Yangpyeong was estimated at about KRW 2 billion.

Lee said the sections, design timing, contractor and cost-sharing should be settled first, so that finished roads are not dug up again. Branch pipes and equipment to reach each village need separate money that financially weak cities and counties cannot easily cover alone, Lee said, calling for a legal basis to use the province's development-gain return fund for gas networks and water supply. The committee said talks are under way with gas suppliers in the province on a model in which a city or county puts in less than KRW 5 billion and the supplier builds the network, and that suppliers have responded positively.

The return fund, built from a share of public development profits, accumulated KRW 150.5 billion from 2021 to 2025. Its first programme, chosen in March this year, gives Paju, Yeoncheon, Yangpyeong and Gapyeong KRW 16 billion each over four years, KRW 64 billion in total, for sports, culture and welfare facilities. Twenty-three cities and counties applied.

Citizen Energy View

A supply station costing about KRW 100 billion and six to seven years of consultation show how long and costly it is for a gas main to reach a village. Residents left without city gas have a fair case, but the pipes laid now would also lock in fossil fuel use for decades. If the development-gain fund is used, the same money should be weighed against village-owned solar and electric heating. The province and the council should publish the costs of village-level energy transition options alongside the pipeline plan.
