Dutch lower house asks government for balcony solar connection framework by spring 2027

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.12

12 of 17 parliamentary groups in favour; 35% of households already have solar

The Dutch House of Representatives has passed a motion asking the government to build a framework by spring 2027 for safely connecting balcony solar to the grid. Felix Klos of Democrats 66 (D66) tabled it on November 5 as an attachment to the 2027 budget for the climate and green growth portfolio. Twelve of the 17 parliamentary groups voted in favour, including D66, the CDA and the VVD.

The motion hands the government two tasks, the connection framework and a list of the standards and rules that would have to change, and it states that 35% of Dutch households have already installed solar. According to the motion, balcony solar lets households without a suitable roof generate their own power and lower their bills. It points to Germany, which changed its standards so that small solar systems can be plugged into a socket under conditions (plug-in systems are small solar units used through a wall socket). In Germany, about 500 MW of the solar capacity added in 2025 came from plug-in systems. Elsewhere in Europe, the UK legalised balcony solar in England, Scotland and Wales in August, and Greece in September allowed plug-in systems up to 800 W and standalone batteries.

News at a glance The motion's original text is posted on the Dutch House of Representatives website (tweedekamer.nl) as budget paper 37 020 XXIII, no. 16, dated November 5. The text sets no capacity cap, installation conditions or start date, and these have not yet been decided. The government's deadline for the framework and the list of rules is spring 2027.
