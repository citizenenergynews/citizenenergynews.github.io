Hanam's Dongseoul Converter Station Agreement Goes to a Gamil Residents' Vote

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.26

The climate ministry, KEPCO and resident representatives agreed on November 25. Heads of households in 19 apartment complexes will vote on signing, and a third-party body will verify electromagnetic and noise data.

Heads of households in all 19 apartment complexes of the Gamil district in Hanam, Gyeonggi Province, will decide by a residents' vote whether to sign an agreement on the Dongseoul converter station project. The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment agreed on a draft agreement with resident representatives at a "thank-you meeting" for the Dongseoul converter station residents' council held at the National Assembly at 3:30 p.m. on November 25. The council, made up of Gamil resident representatives, the ministry, Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) and the office of lawmaker Lee Kwang-jae, held 17 meetings over three months after its launch on August 26.

The residents' representatives raised the question of moving the converter station to another site and concerns about living conditions such as electromagnetic fields and noise. The draft agreement sets out a procedure under which a third-party expert body will verify KEPCO's review of relocating to an alternative site and its safety data on electromagnetic fields, noise and fire. KEPCO will begin the administrative procedures for construction alongside the verification. If the final verification finds a serious defect or hazard, KEPCO will halt the procedures, and the ministry will request follow-up measures and check that they are carried out.

Safety measures include using electromagnetic shielding materials in the station building and having residents and KEPCO jointly check electromagnetic fields and noise after completion. Trees will be planted on the side facing the Gamil district to screen the facility. Resident representatives and KEPCO will form a separate council to work out details of local benefit-sharing measures. Minister of Climate, Energy and Environment Kim Sung-whan said, "It is meaningful that through this council we jointly created a procedure to verify residents' concerns and discussed local benefit-sharing measures."

If the vote passes, the resident representatives, the ministry, KEPCO and Lee Kwang-jae's office will sign, and the agreement will take effect on the day it is signed. The Dongseoul converter station is part of the East Coast–Dongseoul high-voltage direct current (HVDC) transmission project, which links Uljin in North Gyeongsang Province to Hanam in Gyeonggi Province over 280km. In May 2025, KEPCO said it had completed agreements with residents of all 79 villages along the line in North Gyeongsang, Gangwon and Gyeonggi provinces.

Citizen Energy View

Residents designing the verification procedure together over 17 meetings in three months shows a way to resolve conflicts over the power grid. Communities that host transmission lines and converter stations often complain that they bear only the risks and none of the benefits. For benefit-sharing to go beyond one-off payments, lasting shares should be designed, such as local renewable energy projects in which nearby residents invest and share the profits. We hope the new benefit-sharing council between resident representatives and KEPCO puts that discussion first.
