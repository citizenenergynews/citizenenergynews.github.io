Three special bills to merge five power generators filed, with Ulsan and Busan head-office options side by side

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.15

Kim Tae-seon bill puts head office in Ulsan / Lee Heon-seung bill in Busan; Kim Joo-young bill turns KEPCO into a holding company

The Special Act on the Establishment and Support of an Integrated Power Generation Company (bill No. 2222811) was submitted to the National Assembly on Nov. 13. Filed by ten lawmakers including Kim Tae-seon and Kim Gi-hyeon, the bill would create Korea Power Co., all of whose shares would be held by Korea Electric Power Corp. (KEPCO), with its head office in Ulsan. The company would take over all assets, rights and obligations of the five state-run generators split off in the 2001 power-sector restructuring.

Once the minister of climate, energy and environment approves, the corporate merger procedures under the relevant laws would be simplified. Central and local governments could grant the new company tax relief. The bill makes expanding renewables and phasing down coal the company's duties, and leaves drafting the articles of incorporation and recommending executives to an integration promotion committee (a body the bill sets up to handle the establishment work). On the same day, Rep. Lee Heon-seung filed a bill placing the head office in Busan through a new merger, and Rep. Kim Joo-young filed one that turns KEPCO into a power holding company and links electricity rates to costs.

The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment announced on Sept. 4 a plan to merge the same five companies into one. The government aims to pass a special law within this year's regular Assembly session and to launch the company officially on Oct. 1, 2027.

Reading the News The bill was received during the 439th regular session of the Assembly, and its rationale and main provisions can be found under bill No. 2222811. The government plan announced on Sept. 4 would create three to four regional renewable energy divisions and assign about 600 of the roughly 2,400 head-office staff to them to handle local solar and onshore wind projects. Existing regional thermal power divisions would keep their current structure. The location of the merged company's head office has not been decided; the government's stated approach is to weigh current locations, just-transition impact and living and working conditions, and to link the decision to the second round of public-institution relocation.
