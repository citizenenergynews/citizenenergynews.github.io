Soboba Tribal Fire Station Adds a Vanadium Flow Battery to Its Solar Power, Securing at Least 10 Hours of Electricity During Outages

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.11

Extreme fire-threat zone has seen outages of more than 24 hours…0.5 MWh storage system handed over

The fire station of the Soboba Band of Luiseño Indians, near San Jacinto, California, has added a vanadium flow battery to its solar microgrid. During an outage or a utility's wildfire-prevention power shutoff, it can now keep the lights on for at least 10 hours. The area is in the highest wildfire-risk class, Tier 3 (extreme), and has had repeated outages, some lasting more than 24 hours. In a major disaster the station serves as an emergency response facility, an incident command center and a hub for residents.

Invinity, which supplied the equipment, said it delivered three "VS3" flow batteries (batteries that store energy in liquid electrolyte held in tanks) totaling 0.5 MWh, and that the station has 50 kW of solar. The company explains that the electrolyte does not burn, so there is no thermal-runaway risk like that of lithium-ion batteries. GRID Alternatives developed the project with support from the California Energy Commission (CEC), as one of the state's projects to demonstrate long-duration storage that does not rely on lithium-ion. The handover ceremony, attended by the tribe and representatives of both companies, took place one week before the Nov. 10 report.
