Pocheon's 10 RE100 income villages put solar on 160 homes

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.20

Pocheon signed an agreement with 10 villages and two installers on 18 November. KRW 971 million will fund 480 kW on homes and 152 kW of village-owned plants by the end of 2027.

Solar panels for self-consumption will be installed at 160 detached houses in 10 villages of Pocheon, Gyeonggi Province, and shared village power plants will go up at 10 community facilities. The signing ceremony for the 2026 Gyeonggi RE100 Income Village project was held on 18 November at Pocheon City Hall, and the city announced it the next day. The 10 villages and two renewable energy installers joined the agreement. Pocheon City runs the project as the lead agency, with a deadline of 31 December 2027.

The villages are Nogok-ri and Jangam-ri in Idong-myeon; Gilmyeong 3-ri, Suip-ri and Hwadae 1-ri in Ildong-myeon; Idonggyo 3-ri, Jikdong 1-ri and Jikdong 4-ri in Soheul-eup; Jajak 2-tong in Seondan-dong; and Jikdu 1-ri in Gunnae-myeon. Installation will be done by KD Solar and Shinmirae Energy. With a total budget of KRW 971 million, the city will install 480 kW of household solar and 152 kW at community facilities, or about 3 kW per household.

The provincial programme installs solar in energy-poor villages, such as those without city gas, to cut electricity bills and share the income from community plants with residents. In February the province set a target of 200 villages this year with KRW 12.8 billion of provincial money, and a goal of 2,000 villages by 2030. For village projects the province (30%) and the city or county (40%) cover 70% of installation costs, and the Gyeonggi Environment and Energy Agency handles the preparatory procedures in one place.

Machimi village, also in Pocheon, won the top prize in the province's best-practice review last year. Its plant, built with KRW 5.5 million from each of 33 households, earns KRW 80 million a year and pays each household about KRW 200,000 a month, while 68 households have cut their electricity bills to virtually zero. As of September last year Gyeonggi had 473 RE100 villages with 46 MW in total.

Citizen Energy View

Pocheon is putting 632 kW of solar into 10 villages for KRW 971 million. Whether village solar really becomes residents' income is answered by Machimi in the same city, where a KRW 5.5 million stake brings in KRW 200,000 a month. Household systems, however, mainly lower bills; village-wide income comes from the shared plants. Pocheon should help each village set out in its bylaws how the income from the 152 kW of shared plants will be divided, and make that public.
