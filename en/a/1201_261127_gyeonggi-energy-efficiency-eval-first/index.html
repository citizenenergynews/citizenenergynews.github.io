Gyeonggi Tops National Energy Efficiency Evaluation, Up Nine Places in Two Years

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.27

The province scored 78.74 points, ranking 3rd on quantitative and 1st on qualitative measures. Its energy-saving facility loans cut 38,273 toe, the most nationwide, and the efficient-tire rate for public vehicles reached 51.3%.

Gyeonggi Province ranked first overall among Korea's 17 metropolitan and provincial governments in the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment's 2025 evaluation of energy use rationalization performance. The province said on November 27 that it scored 78.74 points, rising nine places in two years from 10th in 2023 and 3rd in 2024.

The evaluation is conducted every year under Article 6 of the Energy Use Rationalization Act and Article 3 of its enforcement decree, rating each province's energy savings and policy results in industry, buildings and transport. The Korea Energy Agency carries it out on the ministry's behalf, adding a quantitative score of 57 points across 10 indicators and a qualitative score of 43 points across 6 indicators. Gyeonggi placed 3rd on the quantitative score with 41.16 points and 1st on the qualitative score with 37.58 points.

In industry, participation in the Korea Energy Efficiency Partnership (KEEP 30) saved 243,824 toe (one toe is the heat of one ton of crude oil). KEEP 30 is a voluntary agreement between the government and sites that use more than 200,000 toe of energy a year. Its goal is an average annual improvement of at least 1% in energy intensity over the five years from 2023 to 2027. According to the ministry, the 30 participating companies saved 968,000 toe nationwide in 2024, with an average energy intensity improvement of 2.6% per company. The energy-saving facility loan program, which lends money to replace old equipment, saved 38,273 toe, the most in the country. Forty small and mid-sized companies took part in a leading-project program. In buildings, the province recorded 213 final and 276 preliminary zero-energy building certifications.

The share of high-efficiency tires on public-agency vehicles reached 51.3%, more than five times the 10% target. Results in the data and new-industry category included an industrial-complex RE100 platform, a shared energy storage (ESS) pilot and a trading platform for renewable power purchase agreements (PPAs).

Citizen Energy View

Gyeonggi ranked first on the qualitative evaluation of policy efforts but third on the quantitative evaluation of actual savings. Quantitative measures account for 57 of the 100 points, and Gyeonggi earned 41.16 of them. In the province's own materials, the industrial-complex RE100 platform and PPA trading support are listed under data and new industries, not under energy savings. The gap between 3rd place on the quantitative score and 1st on the qualitative score is a separate figure to read alongside this first-place result.
