Electric bus fire response: 62 Gyeonggi firefighters train hands-on

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.20

Gyeonggi Technical School drilled high-voltage cut-off and regenerative braking release at Yangpyeong Fire Station on 17 November. Since July, EV fire damage is covered up to 15 billion won per incident.

Sixty-two firefighters in Gyeonggi Province have completed hands-on training to prepare for fires in large electric buses. The training took place at Yangpyeong Fire Station on 17 November, with Gyeonggi Technical School, part of the Gyeonggi Job Foundation, running both the theory and field sessions.

The course was built around large electric buses carrying high-capacity batteries. The foundation said electric bus fires are hard to put out and carry a high risk of reigniting. Firefighters repeatedly practised the response sequence of identifying an electric bus, cutting off high voltage, forcing entry and releasing regenerative braking. They also learned how to wear protective gear and deal with the risk of electric shock.

In the classroom, they studied the main components of electric vehicles, battery locations that differ by model, how fires spread and why they reignite, and reviewed real accident cases. At a bus depot, they inspected hazards inside and outside the vehicles to prepare for call-outs. "We designed practice-centred training so that firefighters can learn the skills actually needed in the field as industry changes," said Heo Jeong-eun, head of Gyeonggi Technical School.

A scheme to cover damage from EV fires was introduced this year. Since 1 July the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment has run an Electric Vehicle Fire Safety Insurance scheme that compensates damage to nearby vehicles or buildings from fires in parked or charging EVs, up to 15 billion won per incident and 45 billion won a year. EVs less than 10 years from first registration are covered even when the cause of the fire is not identified, and victims are paid first, with insurers settling later. The annual premium of 6 billion won is split between the government, which pays 2 billion won, and participating carmakers and importers, which pay 4 billion won, so owners do not need to sign up. The insurance is run by DB Insurance, Hyundai Marine & Fire and Samsung Fire & Marine, and the list of participating companies and the policy terms are posted on the zero-emission vehicle portal (ev.or.kr).

Citizen Energy View

As electric buses and cars multiply, fear of fire becomes a reason to slow the transition. Firefighters learning battery locations and high-voltage cut-off procedures by hand is the surest answer to that fear. Village charging stations built by residents and solar-powered chargers in public car parks need the same safety standards and training if communities are to own the transition. Let us start by checking when our neighbourhood chargers were last inspected.
