Korea and Australia sign decarbonisation MOU, with civil society and youth participation among six areas of cooperation

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.19

Cooperation from electrification and grids to climate adaptation / Joint submission to UNFCCC secretariat lists Korea's 53-61% and Australia's 62-70% reduction targets

South Korea's Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment and Australia's Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water signed a memorandum of understanding on joint decarbonisation pathways on Nov. 17 in Antalya, Türkiye, the host city of the 31st Conference of the Parties to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (COP31). The MOU covers six areas: securing electrification and power grids, technology cooperation and capacity building, creating investment conditions for clean energy and industrial decarbonisation, climate adaptation and resilience, support for implementing nationally determined contributions (NDCs), and wider participation of civil society and young people.

Korean Minister Kim Sung-hwan and Australian Minister Chris Bowen named a green industry initiative, hydrogen certification research, clean energy supply chain research, and a Track 1.5 dialogue involving government and private experts as follow-up tasks in a joint statement the same day. A joint submission the two countries filed with the UNFCCC secretariat lists Korea's 2035 target (a 53-61% cut in net emissions from 2018) alongside Australia's 2035 target (a 62-70% cut from 2005). The MOU breaks down the goals of the Korea-Australia green economy partnership on climate and energy (GEPACE) concluded in 2024 into specific areas and uses the existing ministerial consultation body; the released materials set out no separate budget or project size.

The ministry said Kim plans to attend a ministerial dialogue on implementing the energy transition hosted by the Netherlands, Ireland and Tuvalu on Nov. 18 (local time).

Citizen Energy View

The six areas of the MOU include "wider participation of civil society and young people," but the follow-up tasks in the joint statement, such as green industry, hydrogen certification and supply chain research, centre on governments, companies and experts. As this paper reported on Nov. 15, the same Australian government has agreed with the South Australian government to provide A$17.8 million so that apartment residents can share rooftop solar and batteries. If decarbonisation cooperation is filled only with exports and hydrogen trade, concern may grow that local residents are left with only the burden of power plants and transmission lines; the answer to that concern is a structure in which residents own the facilities directly and share the profits. When the Track 1.5 dialogue is organised, local energy cooperatives and local governments should be included as participants so that the civic participation area is filled with real projects.
