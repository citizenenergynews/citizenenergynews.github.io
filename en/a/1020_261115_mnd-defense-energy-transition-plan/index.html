Defense ministry drafts its first military energy transition plan, aimed at keeping power on during blackouts

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.15

12 detailed tasks derived across four areas…Sangmudae microgrid in Jangseong combines 3,015 kW of solar with 9.76 MWh of storage

South Korea's Ministry of National Defense held a final review meeting on its "defense energy transition plan" on November 12 at its Yongsan headquarters in Seoul, chaired by Vice Minister Lee Doo-hee. The plan, the first the ministry has drawn up at the ministry level, was reviewed in front of about 30 officials from the armed services and related agencies. It covers energy technology to raise the efficiency, output and quiet operation of weapon systems, standardization so equipment works together, design standards for military facilities set through microgrid pilots, and regular progress-review meetings. It also introduces the concept of "mission-critical load" (MCL), meaning equipment that must keep receiving power for a mission even during a blackout.

The ministry cited rising power demand from high-energy weapons such as lasers and electromagnetic systems as the background. It also named mobility, meaning units securing their own power while moving and deploying, and sustainability, meaning continuing operations without outside support. Lee said, "Energy security is national security and a core element tied directly to the military's survivability and combat power." The ministry plans to settle short-term and mid-to-long-term roadmaps based on the 12 detailed tasks in four areas and to build a cooperation framework among the services and related agencies.

The military's own grid will be tested at Sangmudae in Jangseong, South Jeolla. The Korea Energy Agency, commissioned by the Army with 20 billion won, is building an integrated microgrid (a small, independent grid that generates, stores and supplies power from its own sources and storage). The fixed facilities are 3,015 kW of solar, 9.76 MWh of energy storage (ESS) and 4 MW of diesel generators, joined by mobile solar units and vehicle-mounted storage. The agency plans five years of testing after construction and expects energy self-sufficiency of 16.1%. The agency said it signed the commissioning agreement with the Army on February 26 this year, and the agreement runs for 12 months from the signing date.

Reading the News The body of the ministry's press release does not give the names of the 12 tasks, the funding required or a year-by-year schedule. The full release and its PDF and HWPX attachments are available on the government's Policy Briefing site (korea.kr). The release does not state when the roadmap will be finalized.
