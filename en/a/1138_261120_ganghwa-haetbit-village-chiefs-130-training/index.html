Over 130 Ganghwa village heads trained on 'sunlight income villages'

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.20

Incheon's Ganghwa County taught site selection, permits and resident acceptance at the county office on November 16. In the ministry's second call, Incheon had one of two applicant villages chosen.

More than 130 village heads in Ganghwa County, Incheon, learned how to run village solar projects themselves. The county said on the 19th that it held a training session for village heads on "resident-led sunlight income villages" at Jindallae Hall in the county office on November 16.

The lecture was given by an instructor from a team of specialist trainers for the sunlight income village program. It covered the purpose and background of the project and cases from other villages, followed by the application procedure, how to choose a site, points to watch in permitting and ways to build resident acceptance. The village heads looked together at how projects actually operate and how revenue has been used, and weighed whether the model fits their own villages. County Governor Park Yong-cheol asked them to choose sites carefully so that they suit Ganghwa as a tourist city and protect the landscape.

At its core, a sunlight income village is a residents' cooperative that builds solar panels on idle village land and puts the money from selling electricity toward residents' income and village welfare. In the second call announced by the Ministry of the Interior and Safety on October 8, 608 villages nationwide applied and 436 were chosen; in Incheon, two villages applied and one was chosen. By region, Jeonnam-Gwangju had the most, with 106 chosen out of 169 applicants. Of the 436, 23 villages were classed as ready to start right away, with a cooperative, site and financing plan in place. The other 413 were conditionally selected and must still settle site lease talks, financing plans or grid connection. The 160 villages that had not secured a site or had not submitted a financing plan were told to apply again in a later call.

Adding the 87 villages chosen in the first round, 523 villages have been selected nationwide. The ministry took applications for a third call from September 17 to October 30 and plans to announce results at the end of December. Selected villages receive help with permits such as generation business licenses, along with financing support of up to 85%.

Citizen Energy View

In the second call, Incheon had one of two applicant villages chosen, while 160 villages nationwide were pushed to a later round for lack of preparation such as a site or financing plan. Whether a village project succeeds depends less on one village head's will than on whether all residents know the same facts. Villages looking for sites that protect the landscape can start with land already in use, such as the roofs of village halls and warehouses or parking lots. Village heads should share what they learned with every resident at the village assembly.
