Seoul's Dobong-gu Surveys Demand for Solar Subsidies Until December 11

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.25

The 2028 project, to be submitted to a national funding competition, covers 70-80% of installation costs. Owners of houses, apartments and commercial buildings can apply; tenants cannot.

Seoul's Dobong-gu district is taking early applications until December 11 from homes and buildings that want to join its 2028 solar program. The district posted the demand survey on its website on November 17 and said national and local government funds would cover about 70-80% of installation costs. Applicants pay the remaining 20-30%, which the district estimates they can recover in about three years through lower electricity bills.

The survey is an advance recruitment for a renewable energy competition, including solar power, run by the Korea Energy Agency. Based on the results, the district will submit a project to the agency's competition in May-June 2027 and, if selected, install the systems in 2028. Eligible systems include solar power, solar thermal and geothermal, and the applicant's share may rise or fall depending on the national funding rate. Through this competition, whose application form is titled "Solar (Convergence) Distribution Program," Incheon's Yeongjong-gu will spend a total of 4.19 billion won at 227 sites in the district in 2027.

Owners of detached houses, apartment common facilities, general buildings such as shops and factories, and public buildings in Dobong-gu can apply. Tenants cannot apply, and balcony solar panels for individual households are excluded from this survey because a separate program is planned for 2027. The application asks for the roof type (flat slab or pitched), installation type (fixed) and desired capacity in kW. Applying does not guarantee installation; whether a system is installed and its size depend on the 2027 competition results and site conditions.

Applications can be made by phone to the district's Climate and Environment Division, or by delivering or mailing the form to the division on the third floor of the district office at 656 Madeul-ro. The district will also hold briefings by visiting apartment residents' representative councils and building owners. The deadline is Friday, December 11.

Citizen Energy View

If the public sector pays 70-80% of installation costs and owners recover their share in about three years, the terms are not bad for those with a roof. But tenants cannot apply, so residents who rent their homes are left out of this support. If apartment common facilities and public building roofs were combined into power plants that residents invest in together, tenants could share the returns too. Before the 2027 competition, the district should also present a plan to open public building roofs to citizen-funded power plants.
