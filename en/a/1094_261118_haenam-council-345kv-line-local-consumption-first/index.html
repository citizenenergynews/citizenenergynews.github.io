Haenam council and farmers' groups demand local-use system before Sinhaenam–Singangjin 345kV line

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.18

29.2km and 79 towers of the 36.8km line cross Haenam, with completion targeted for December 2030 / Renewable self-sufficient city act and RE100 national industrial complex should come first, groups say

The Agriculture, Fisheries and Economy Committee of Haenam County Council held a meeting on Nov. 16 in the council's resident communication room with representatives of farmers' groups and officials from Korea Electric Power Corporation's Gwangju-Jeonnam construction office on the 345kV Sinhaenam–Singangjin transmission line project. The line will link the new Sinhaenam and Singangjin substations over 36.8km (96 towers), of which 29.2km (79 towers) runs through Haenam, with completion targeted for December 2030.

Representatives of four groups, including the Haenam County Farmers' Association, said they oppose sending electricity generated in Haenam to the Seoul metropolitan area and called for a "local production, local consumption" system to be put in place before any transmission line. As conditions, the groups named the enactment of a special act on renewable energy self-sufficient cities and the designation of an RE100 national industrial complex in Haenam, and the eight county councillors present said the council would respond. KEPCO officials attended, but the council's press release does not include KEPCO's account.

Bills titled the "Special Act on the Creation of and Support for Renewable Energy Self-Sufficient Cities," including ones introduced on Nov. 14, 2025 (by Rep. Shin Young-dae and 22 others) and Aug. 10, 2026 (by Rep. Kim Eui-kyeom and 13 others), are pending before the National Assembly's Trade, Industry, Energy, SMEs and Startups Committee.

Citizen Energy View

Of the 36.8km Sinhaenam–Singangjin line, 29.2km, roughly four-fifths, crosses Haenam land. As long as villages living under the towers bear the burden while being left out of the benefits of the power, opposition will recur line after line. Alongside using Haenam's electricity in Haenam, villagers along the route should be given a way to hold stakes in local renewable power plants. The National Assembly should start reviewing the special act that has sat in committee for more than a year, and the government and KEPCO should present plans for resident participation along the route together with their transmission plans.
