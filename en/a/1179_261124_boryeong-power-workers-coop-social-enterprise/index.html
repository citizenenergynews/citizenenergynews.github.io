Boryeong power plant workers' cooperative named a pre-social enterprise in South Chungcheong

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.24

The renewable energy cooperative was founded in August by non-regular power plant workers in response to coal plant closures. The province designated 17 companies, which get three years of fund eligibility and priority purchasing support.

The Power Plant Workers' Renewable Energy Cooperative in Boryeong, South Chungcheong, has been designated a pre-social enterprise by South Chungcheong Province. The province said on November 23 that it held its second pre-social enterprise designation review committee and finally designated 17 companies, including the cooperative. Non-regular power plant workers founded the cooperative in August this year in response to the closure of coal-fired power plants. The Special Act on Support for Coal-Fired Power Plant Workers and Closure Areas was promulgated on September 15 and takes effect on September 16, 2027.

The cooperative earns income from building and operating solar power plants, maintaining renewable energy equipment and training specialists. Its goal is to reinvest that income in job-transition training for workers and in creating jobs. In September it won a contract for a pilot project maintaining renewable energy equipment at public institutions in the Boryeong area and is now carrying it out. It has no employees yet but plans to increase its workforce to 6 in 2027, 12 in 2028 and 25 in 2029.

A pre-social enterprise meets the minimum legal requirements for certification but falls short on some, such as its revenue structure (once it meets them, it is certified as a social enterprise). Designated companies can apply to the Chungnam social economy fund for three years and also receive support to move into the Chungnam Social Economy Innovation Town in Cheongyang. They become priority purchasing targets for public institutions and receive development consulting and distribution and sales support.

This round of designations also included Ongiro in Hongseong, which was founded in August, renovated an empty house in Ogwan-ri, Hongseong-eup, and opened its first rural guesthouse, Ongiro Stay, on October 17. Bailey in Gongju, which makes plant-based synthetic leather from chestnut inner skins (yulpi), was also designated. Bailey has hired one worker since its founding in July and is pursuing a patent application for its method of making chestnut-skin synthetic leather, as well as overseas sales through Amazon.

Citizen Energy View

When coal-fired power plants close, non-regular workers are the first to lose their jobs. That these workers have formed a cooperative themselves to move into renewable energy construction and maintenance shows that a just transition can be a business, not just words. For the plan to employ 25 people by 2029 to come true, the public-institution pilot contract won in September must not be a one-off. South Chungcheong Province and Boryeong City should open the way to steadily entrust public solar maintenance work to local worker cooperatives.
