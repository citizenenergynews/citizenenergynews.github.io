Gyeonggi checks wiring and fire safety in 1,622 ageing rental flats

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.20

Gyeonggi Province, the Korea Electrical Safety Corporation and fire stations are inspecting homes in three public rental complexes from 18 to 25 November, replacing faulty breakers, with regular joint checks from 2027.

Gyeonggi Province is inspecting the electrical and fire safety equipment inside homes at three ageing public rental apartment complexes in Suwon and Osan, covering 1,622 households. The province began the first inspection on 18 November together with the Gyeonggi branch of the Korea Electrical Safety Corporation, the Suwon and Osan fire stations and the Korea Housing Management Corporation. The checks aim to prevent electrical fires in winter at complexes where many older people, people with disabilities and other vulnerable residents live.

The first day, the 18th, covered Maetan 6 complex in Suwon (710 households), followed by Unam 6 complex in Osan (571 households) on the 23rd and Baekseol Maeul in Suwon (341 households) on the 25th. Teams stay at each complex from 10am to 2pm and enter only homes whose residents have agreed. About 30 staff from the province, fire stations, the Korea Electrical Safety Corporation and other bodies take part each time.

The Korea Electrical Safety Corporation sends around 20 inspectors to check whether residual-current breakers work properly and what condition the indoor wiring is in. Where needed, they carry out minor repairs such as replacing breakers or switches, and give electrical safety lessons. Fire stations check extinguishers and fire detectors and explain what to do in the first moments of a fire. The province handles notices to residents and coordination between the agencies.

Based on the risks and improvement tasks found this time, the province will set up a regular joint inspection system with the agencies for 2027 to 2029. It is also preparing a field-based approach that runs from identifying high-risk households to in-home checks, repairing dangerous facilities and follow-up checks. The Ministry of the Interior and Safety and the National Fire Agency have designated 1 November to 28 February next year as the winter fire safety period, during which local governments are to inspect fire-vulnerable facilities such as old housing together with fire, electricity and gas bodies. Fires in winter averaged about 11,000 a year from 2021 to 2025, 28% of all fires, the ministry said.

Citizen Energy View

As policies to switch heating to electricity spread, the wiring and breakers inside old apartments become a hidden weak link in the energy transition. Checks that go inside each home, like these, are fire prevention and also the starting point of energy welfare. Rather than stopping at inspection, the work should grow into a programme that bundles rewiring, efficiency upgrades and even rooftop solar to cut electricity bills for rental tenants. The regular inspection system to be built from 2027 should also leave room for residents to take part.
