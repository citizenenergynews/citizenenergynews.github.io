Bill would double the cap on Korea's domestic production tax credit for solar and wind

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.19

The amendment filed by Rep. Lim Ho-seon and 10 other lawmakers counts output from preferred non-capital regions at 1.5 times the credit. Public comment runs until November 22.

A bill to raise the total cap of Korea's planned domestic production tax credit to 100% of cumulative investment has been submitted to the National Assembly. The credit cuts corporate or income tax for companies that make and sell items such as solar and wind power products in Korea. Rep. Lim Ho-seon and 10 other lawmakers filed the amendment to the Restriction of Special Taxation Act on November 12, and it sits with the Fiscal, Economic and Planning Committee. According to the bill's explanatory note, the government's plan sets the 10-year total cap at 50% of cumulative investment in business assets used directly in production.

Eligible items are those designated by presidential decree in six fields: solar power, wind power, secondary batteries, semiconductors, core materials and AI robot parts (which items qualify has not yet been decided). The credit equals a per-item amount set by decree multiplied by sales volume, then by one of four regional rates, from 100% in the capital area to 150% in preferred areas of non-capital general regions. The rate is 100% for tax years 2027 through 2033, then falls to 75% in 2034, 50% in 2035 and 25% in 2036. The credit in any year cannot exceed 50% of that year's costs directly tied to production.

The bill also applies the regional rates to the total cap, so output made outside the capital area gets a larger cap. It drops the requirement that items be sold in the year they are made or the following year. Facilities that received the integrated investment tax credit in tax years starting on or before December 31, 2026 can also claim the new credit if the company cancels that earlier credit through an amended return. In that case the company repays the credit with interest, but no penalty tax is added. The Minister of Finance and Economy may ask companies for detailed production cost and price data, and trade secrets and similar material will not be disclosed.

The law would take effect on January 1, 2027. The National Assembly's public comment period runs from November 13 to 22, and comments can be filed on its legislative notice website.

Citizen Energy View

This credit goes to manufacturers running solar and wind component factories. Giving a bigger credit the further production is from the capital is a device to keep the production base in the regions. If taxpayers are to prop up domestic equipment, there should also be a way to link public procurement with resident-participation projects so that this equipment goes first to Sunshine Income Villages and energy cooperative power plants. We hope the committee's review treats production support and wider local ownership as one package.
