Gyeonggi's 101% public-sector renewable figure counts capacity; actual output is 14% of use

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.15

Target met with 28.5MW of installed solar…2025 generation 8,543MWh against 60,823MWh of power use

The 101% that Gyeonggi Province cites for exceeding 100% renewable power in its public sector is measured by installed solar capacity, not by actual generation. On April 29 the province said it had built 28.5MW, above the 28.2MW target that matches the electricity consumption of the province and its 28 public agencies (capacity is the size of the plants built; generation is the power they actually produce).

Ahn Gye-myeong of the Gyeonggi Provincial Council's Urban Environment Committee (Democratic Party, Pyeongtaek 5) pointed to the gap on November 12 at an administrative audit of the Climate, Environment and Energy Bureau. In data the province submitted, 2025 electricity use by the province and public agencies was 60,823 MWh and renewable generation was 8,543 MWh, about 14% of use. Ahn said wording such as '101% renewable production' in provincial press releases could be read as actual usage levels, and asked the province to publish its calculation basis alongside actual generation.

The province declared its Gyeonggi RE100 vision in April 2023 and has pursued a plan to cover public-sector power use with renewables within 2026. In its April announcement it said it would extend the approach to 31 cities and counties from the second half of 2026.

Reading the News The province's press release (No. 70214) says it built a 1.5MW building-integrated solar cluster at Gyeonggi Convergence Town and converted idle land around the Seosuwon and Wolam interchanges into resident-participation power plants. Heads of public agencies now have a renewable-achievement indicator in their performance reviews. The usage and generation figures from the council audit follow Gimpo Citizen News; this paper could not open the original data the province submitted to the council.
