Two Villages Near Perleberg, Germany: Developer Drops 41-Hectare Solar Plan After Residents' Poll

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.11

At the 5 October poll, 8 of 11 present in Dergenthin opposed; in Sükow, 19 of 35 opposed and 10 supported. The result is not legally binding

Plans for ground-mounted solar on farmland in two villages of the town of Perleberg, in the Prignitz area of Brandenburg, Germany, have stalled after a residents' poll. The developer, SolarWind Projekt, asked the town on 5 December 2025 about 41 hectares (410,000 square metres) of farmland: 35 hectares in Sükow and 6 in Dergenthin.

After an internal preliminary review, the town held a residents' assembly on 5 October, where the developer's side explained how the plants would be built and a poll was taken. In Dergenthin, 8 of the 11 people present opposed and 3 abstained, with no one in favour; in Sükow, 10 of 35 supported, 19 opposed and 6 abstained. The result is not legally binding and the plans could have gone on, but a town administration document records that the developer will not pursue the project further.

Section 6(3) of Germany's Renewable Energy Sources Act (EEG 2023) says operators of ground-mounted solar may offer the host municipality a total of up to 0.2 cents per kWh of electricity actually fed into the grid. In Schilde, also in the Prignitz area, about 80% of those present opposed a new solar park in a residents' poll in September 2026.
