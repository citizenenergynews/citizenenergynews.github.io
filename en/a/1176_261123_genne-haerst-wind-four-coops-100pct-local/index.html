Four Dutch co-ops push for 100% locally owned wind farm

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.23

Overijssel province signed a development agreement for the Genne-Haerst wind farm on November 20. A residents' drop-in on a five-turbine layout will be held in Zwolle on the 25th.

The Dutch province of Overijssel on November 20 signed a development agreement for the Genne-Haerst wind farm, which four local energy cooperatives are pursuing. The agreement is an "anterieure overeenkomst," a prior agreement that sets out in advance the commitments for the rest of the development process. Provincial executive Tijs de Bree signed for the province, and the other party is the cooperative DuurSaam BuurtWint. The project is being run jointly by that cooperative, Energiek Zwartewaterland, Blauwvinger Energie and Energiecoöperatie Berkum.

The four cooperatives have set a goal of keeping the wind farm 100% locally owned so that decision-making and revenue stay in the area as much as possible. They say the share the province pledged toward national targets in its Regional Energy Strategy (RES) is also a starting point for the project. In November 2025 the province named DuurSaam BuurtWint's operating company, owned by the four cooperatives, as the only party allowed to apply for the wind farm's environmental permit. The layout that scored highest in the province's assessment has five turbines, but the cooperatives say it is not final.

Studies on noise, nature, landscape and shadow flicker will now be carried out to compare several layouts. The cooperatives say the turbines will be at least 400m from any home. Under Dutch government rules, a home may not be hit by shadow flicker for more than six hours a year or 20 minutes a day.

Not only cooperative members but also nearby residents and local people and businesses can put money into the development through bond-style loans. At financial close, lenders get their principal back plus a 50% success fee, but the cooperative says they could also lose their money. A residents' drop-in will be held from 7 p.m. to 9 p.m. on November 25 at the De Weijenbelt community centre in Zwolle, with no registration needed.

Citizen Energy View

Four cooperatives have set out to own a five-turbine wind farm outright, and the province cleared the way by making their company the only permit applicant. Residents put in money from the development stage and give their views at a drop-in before the layout is fixed. In Korea, wind projects are often developed by outside companies, with residents only sharing part of the profits. Provincial and local governments should find ways to give residents' cooperatives first chance at sites and permits so they can own the projects.
