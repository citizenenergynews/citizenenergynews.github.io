Georgia's Coastal Electric Cooperative to Return $1,534,289.65 to Members Who Used Power in 2011–2013

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.11

Checks of $50 or more go out in early December; smaller amounts are deducted from bills starting in November…more than $23 million returned so far

Coastal Electric Cooperative, which supplies power in Bryan, Liberty, Long and McIntosh counties on the Georgia coast, will return $1,534,289.65 to members who used electricity in 2011–2013. Its member-elected board decided on the payout at its October 28 meeting. The money is paid out as capital credits.

Capital credits represent a member's ownership stake in the cooperative and are calculated by multiplying a member's bills for a year by a set percentage. The board decides to return them when the co-op's finances allow, and they cannot be used to pay current bills. Former members who moved away can also receive their share for the years they were served, so they need to keep their mailing address up to date with the co-op.

The cooperative has returned more than $23 million in capital credits so far, and it has more than 20,000 members. Amounts of $50 or more arrive by check in early December, while amounts under $50 are deducted from electric bills starting in November.

Citizen Energy View

This payout covers 2011–2013 usage, so it is paid 13 to 15 years after the years in which the power was used. Coastal Electric describes providing electricity at cost as the reason the co-op exists, and members' stakes stay on its books until the board decides, based on its finances, to return them.
