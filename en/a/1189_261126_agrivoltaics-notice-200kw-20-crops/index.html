Korea Proposes 200kW Cap per Farmer for Agrivoltaics, Limits Crops to 20

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.26

The Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs pre-announced the notice on capacity and crops on November 25. Only 20 crops including rice, soybeans and apples would be allowed; comments are open until December 7.

South Korea's Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs on November 25 pre-announced a draft notice setting the scale of agrivoltaic (farming-plus-solar) projects and the crops that may be grown under them. Projects run by farmers would be capped at 200kW of generating capacity per project, and only 20 crops, including rice, soybeans and potatoes, could be grown on project sites. The ministry's Rural Energy Policy Division will accept comments by mail, email or fax until December 7.

The cap applies not to each installation but to each project for which a permit is sought (if a project includes several installations, their capacities are added together). For a change permit, the capacity after the change is used. Mayors and county or district heads may set a limit lower than 200kW, taking into account the applicant's overall farm size, labor and access to farm machinery. When reducing the size of a project site, they must base their review on the type, timing and volume of farm work for each crop, and also consider irrigation, drainage and access for farm machinery.

The permitted crops are rice, corn, soybeans (including bean sprout soybeans), garlic, onions, potatoes, ginger, lettuce, spinach and cabbage. Strawberries, pears, grapes, apples, tea plants (for green tea only), gang-hwal, ji-hwang, gondalbi, gomchwi and mountain garlic are also on the list. Even listed crops may not be grown if they fall under restrictions set by the enforcement decree. The ministry may add crops whose suitability is confirmed through field trials and cultivation tests, and local governments, research institutes, universities and companies may also propose additions. Any change to the cap or the crop list must be reviewed by an expert council of up to nine members.

The draft enforcement decree, pre-announced on September 16, limits the project period to a maximum of 23 years and requires farming on at least 80% of the project site. The notice takes effect on the day it is issued, and the underlying Act on the Promotion of and Support for Agrivoltaic Power Generation Projects takes effect on December 17.

Citizen Energy View

The 200kW cap per project is a safeguard against agrivoltaics turning into large power plants that use farming as a pretext. But a single farmer's project leaves little for the village as a whole. Since the draft decree requires resident-participation cooperatives to draw up plans for returning profits to residents, pooling village farmland into a cooperative is the way to broaden rural income. Farmers and villages should send their views from the field on the crop list and the cap by December 7.
