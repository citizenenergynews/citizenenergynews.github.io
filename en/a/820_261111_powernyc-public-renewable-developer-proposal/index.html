Proposal for a Public Renewable Developer in New York City Estimates 200MW of Batteries Could Save $170 Million a Year

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.11

Three groups led by the Climate and Community Institute released the report on October 28, urging the city to build batteries, solar and thermal energy networks on school, hospital and public housing sites and to ask the state for $100 million in initial capital

A proposal has emerged for New York City to set up a city-owned renewable energy developer that would build distributed batteries, solar and thermal energy networks itself. It comes from the report "PowerNYC Development Corporation," which the Climate and Community Institute issued on October 28 together with the Public Renewables Project and Public Power NY. The report says thousands of viable sites at schools, hospitals and public housing go unbuilt because no one is in a position to handle the technical review, financing and contracts.

The developer would build projects at no upfront cost to New Yorkers and use operating revenue to fund the next ones. For a first project the report proposes 200MW of distributed batteries and estimates that Con Edison ratepayers could save at least $170 million a year (the capacity market sets the price of supply capacity held for periods of peak demand, and the figure counts only savings there). The report recommends that the city ask New York State for $100 million in initial capital.

According to material cited in a footnote of the report, New York City's monthly capacity auction price more than doubled, from about $15 per kilowatt-month (kW-month) in May 2025 to $32.59 in May 2026.
