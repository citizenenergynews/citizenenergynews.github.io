Sakata Agrivoltaics Symposium Hears from Farmer Growing Rice Under 670 Panels Above His Paddy

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.10

Japan's farmland temporary-conversion permits total 5,351 through fiscal 2022…Korea's agrivoltaics act is due to take effect in December

A symposium on agrivoltaics was held on 7 November in Sakata, Yamagata Prefecture, Japan. At the event, hosted by the community-development NPO Hirata Satoyama no Kai, farmers and researchers discussed what power generation above farmland could offer hilly and mountainous areas that people have left.

Yonezawa farmer Seiichi Kimura installed 670 manually adjustable panels, designed for heavy-snow areas, over 35 ares (1 are is 100 square metres) of paddy. Beneath them he grows Haenuki, Yamagata Prefecture's flagship rice variety. Having given up dairy farming at 60 to switch to this approach, he said the up-front investment is substantial but that, beyond a certain scale, it can become one pillar of a farm's income. The head of a farm-power company in Nihonmatsu, Fukushima Prefecture, quit farming after the nuclear accident and returned through agrivoltaics. The company head explained that farmers have started to take an interest in energy. A professor at Tohoku University of Art and Design pointed out that selling electricity and produce together as a village creates ties with the outside world.

According to a May 2025 explanatory document from Japan's Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, temporary farmland-conversion permits for installing such systems totalled 5,351 through fiscal 2022. The farmland beneath the equipment covers 1,209.3 hectares, and permits in fiscal 2022 alone numbered 975. Korea's Act on the Promotion and Support of Agrivoltaic Power Generation Projects was enacted in June. The Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs said in a 8 September explanatory document that the act is scheduled to take effect in December.
