Edinburgh community solar co-op seeks £1.13 million from citizens

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.19

£311,480 had been raised as of 19 November and the offer closes on 15 December. The money funds 1.23MW on 10 public roofs, including five schools; the minimum investment is £25.

A citizen-owned solar co-operative in Edinburgh, Scotland, is raising £1.13 million from local investors to pay for solar panels on the roofs of 10 public buildings. Edinburgh Community Solar Co-operative opened its third share offer on 2 November, and £311,480 had been raised as of 19 November.

The sites are nine council buildings and one community building, including the Royal Commonwealth Pool and five schools. The installations total 1.23MW, and the co-op projects annual generation of 991MWh, enough for the equivalent of 394 medium-sized homes. Investments range from £25 to £50,000, and the co-op intends to pay 5% annual interest, which is not guaranteed. If the offer is oversubscribed, priority goes to Edinburgh residents, then existing members, then residents of Scotland.

Seven of the 10 systems are installed and generating. Of the two remaining schools, one is under way and the other is scheduled for Easter 2027, while the Royal Commonwealth Pool is due in November. A structural review commissioned for the council ruled out one roof and slightly reduced two others. North Edinburgh Childcare had panels fitted in August but closed on 30 October and entered insolvency proceedings, so the co-op removed that income from its projections.

The co-op signed a 20-year fixed-price power purchase agreement with the council in 2015 covering 24 buildings, and added seven more buildings in 2020. By the co-op's estimate, its existing sites have cut more than 2,000 tonnes of CO2 and saved the council almost £850,000 on electricity. Phase 1 shares were paid 6.5% interest in September 2026 and Phase 2 shares 4.5%. The co-op has 667 members and has distributed £108,577 to local groups through its community benefit fund. Applications are open on the Energy4All website until 15 December.

Citizen Energy View

The Edinburgh co-op has used citizen investment to put solar on 30 public sites, and by its estimate the council has saved almost £850,000 on electricity. When a city offers its roofs and residents provide the capital, residents earn interest, public bodies pay less for power, and part of the surplus goes back to neighbourhood groups. Even when a risk materialises, such as the childcare centre's insolvency, disclosing it in the offer document and removing it from the projections is how citizen investment keeps public trust. Korean local governments should consider opening school and sports-facility roofs to local co-operatives first in their public-building solar plans.
