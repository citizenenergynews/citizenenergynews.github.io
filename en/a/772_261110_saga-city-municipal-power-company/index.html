Japan's Saga City Sets Up Local Power Company to Sell Waste-Plant Electricity, Holding 52%

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.10

About 16,000 MWh a year bound for 113 public facilities will be sold by a city-backed firm; supply to start in 2027

Saga City in Japan has set up a local power company that will sell electricity from the city's waste incineration plant directly to public facilities in the city. The company, Saga Junkan Energy (さが循環エナジー), is a third-sector firm (jointly funded by a local government and private companies) backed by the city and six private companies; it was established on 30 October and announced on 9 November. Of its ¥50 million in capital, the city put in ¥26 million (52%) and six companies including Saga Gas (佐賀ガス) put in ¥4 million each.

The plant generates about 30,000 MWh a year from the heat of burning waste and sends about 16,000 MWh, after its own use, to 113 public facilities (1 MWh equals 1,000 kWh). Until now an outside company handled sales of that power and part of the bill left the area; the new company will sell it to public facilities in the city. The president is Kazuyoshi Ikeda, a former deputy mayor involved since the planning stage, who said he wants to build a company that rests on the power business, works with the city's decarbonisation efforts and helps solve local problems (as reported by the Saga Shimbun). The Saga Shimbun put the start of supply at June 2027, while the joint announcement says the first half of fiscal 2027 (April to September).

Citizen Energy View

Saga City has sent the plant's electricity to public facilities while leaving sales to a company outside the city. In the new company the city is the largest investor and handles sales. The publicly named investors number seven: the city and six private companies.
