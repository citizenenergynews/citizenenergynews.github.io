Gyeonggi Cuts Sunlight Self-Reliant Village Budget by Four-Fifths

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.25

Provincial funding falls from 12.837 billion won to 2.543 billion won, and city gas and LPG aid is dropped. The council votes on the budget on December 11.

Gyeonggi Province's 2027 budget proposal cuts provincial funding for three programs that support energy-poor areas by 15.87 billion won from this year. Ahn Gye-myeong (Democratic Party, Pyeongtaek 5), a member of the Gyeonggi Provincial Council's Urban Environment Committee, questioned the cuts on November 24 while reviewing the province's third supplementary budget for 2026 and its main budget for 2027. The "Gyeonggi Sunlight Self-Reliant Village" program falls from 12.837 billion won in this year's main budget to 2.543 billion won next year, about one-fifth, and both the scale of support and the provincial subsidy rate were lowered.

The "city gas pipeline support program" (3.25 billion won) and the "small LPG storage tank program" (2.325 billion won) received no provincial money at all for next year. Twenty-five cities and counties in the province have applied for gas pipeline support on 95 sections. The province ended its own LPG tank program on the grounds that it duplicated a national one, but national support next year covers only one village, while 30 or more villages have applied every year. "Differences in energy supply conditions are turning into heating and electricity bills for residents," Ahn said.

On the same day, council member Lim Yu-jin (Democratic Party, Goyang 1) pointed out that Goyang's shared energy storage system (ESS) pilot is not running properly even though the facility has been built. When they signed an agreement in July 2025, Gyeonggi Province, Goyang City, Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) and LS Electric planned to install 5 MWh of batteries with a total budget of 3.2 billion won and test them for two years. In a shared ESS, several institutions use one battery bank to ease grid pressure and cut electricity bills. The project won a government regulatory exemption in June and part of the facility was completed in September, but talks with KEPCO on power trading and bill settlement have been delayed.

Next year's proposal adds a new "shared ESS pilot for industrial facilities" costing 2 billion won in provincial funds. Lim asked the province to check grid connection, private operators' ability to pay their share and actual operating revenue before choosing sites. The 2027 budget proposal will be reviewed by the Special Committee on Budget and Accounts and taken up at the fifth plenary session on December 11.

Citizen Energy View

The sunlight self-reliant villages and the gas pipeline program both serve rural residents left out of energy supply, yet both were cut at once in Gyeonggi's 2027 budget. Tight finances are understandable, but they do not change the fact that villages that could save on power bills with solar will have to wait longer. Goyang's shared ESS, built but not running properly because settlement talks have stalled, shows that the rules should come before new programs are added. Residents, cities and counties should press the budget committee to revisit the village budget before the December 11 vote and to put energy projects owned directly by residents and villages at the front of the line.
