Korea's second ESS call targets 34 congested distribution lines

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.19

The climate ministry is seeking virtual power plant operators. 1,663 solar plants are waiting to connect on these lines, and applications are taken by email until 4 p.m. on November 27.

Korea's Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment on November 17 reissued, with amendments, the second call for its 2026 AI-Based ESS Deployment Support Project, which seeks operators to install energy storage systems (ESS) on distribution lines where renewable connections are backed up. The amended notice (Ministry Notice No. 2026-1039), posted by the Korea Energy Agency as the managing body, says it changed part of the support terms, including extending the application period. Applications are taken by email to the agency until 4 p.m. on November 27.

This year's national funding is 76.478 billion won, combining 17.928 billion won left over from the first round and 58.55 billion won from a supplementary budget. The government covers up to 50% of total project costs, capped at 586 million won per MWh of rated discharge capacity. Each line is to be built on the basis of 4MW/20MWh.

The call covers 34 distribution lines in Gangwon, North Gyeongsang, South Gyeongsang, South Chungcheong, Gwangju-South Jeolla and North Jeolla, where 1,663 solar plants totaling 366,131kW are waiting to connect. Gwangju-South Jeolla has the most lines at 18, and Haenam County and Gimje City have four each. Applicants must be virtual power plant (VPP, a business that bundles scattered generators and runs them as one) operators registered under the Electricity Business Act, or consortiums that include one, and cooperatives and nonprofit corporations may join consortiums. Local governments cannot take part; each applicant may apply for two to five lines per region and substation, and only the top scorer is chosen for each line.

Newly connected renewables must take part in the power market through the VPP and agree to output curtailment. The equipment must run for 20 years after commercial operation, and at least 15% of the private share must come from equity. Earlier, on November 13, the agency announced a community solar project to install storage on distribution lines serving sunlight income villages, with the same cap of 586 million won per MWh. The second ESS call will notify selection results in December after document reviews and presentations in November and December.

Citizen Energy View

On these 34 lines alone, 1,663 solar plants cannot connect because the lines are full. Building storage to connect them cannot wait, but since support goes to VPP operators, the resident and cooperative plants that have been waiting to connect must not be pushed to the back when revenue is shared. The notice includes a standard revenue-sharing plan among its evaluation items, and cooperatives can join consortiums. Citizen power cooperatives and local governments in the affected areas should work out how to take part with VPP operators before the November 27 deadline.
