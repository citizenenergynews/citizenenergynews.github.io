Gangwon Provincial Council Asks Province to Confirm Grid Access Early for 46 Sunlight Income Villages

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.11

Councillor Lee Byeong-gu reviews grid connection for the 92.4 billion won programme at the November 10 industry bureau audit

Gangwon Special Self-Governing Province Councillor Lee Byeong-gu (이병구, Yanggu) asked the province on November 10, at an administrative audit of the industry bureau, to confirm in advance whether Sunlight Income Villages (햇빛소득마을) can connect to the power grid. Lee said that even a finished plant has trouble operating stably and earning income if its output cannot be tied into the transmission network. This year the province plans to spend a total of 92.4 billion won on village-scale solar plants in 46 villages across 12 cities and counties, and to support solar equipment and energy storage systems (ESS).

In the publicly led onshore wind siting plan, the Daeamsan (대암산) area of Yanggu (양구) County was named as a place that could use wind resources, and opinions were raised that it should be reviewed carefully in light of local conditions and the natural environment. The province will push resident-participation wind farms through 2030 and plans to choose sites by weighing wind conditions, environmental impact, local acceptance and grid capacity together.

According to first-round results the Ministry of the Interior and Safety posted on its Policy Briefing site (정책브리핑) on August 3, 86 of 129 applicant villages were selected; 9 can proceed immediately and 77 were conditionally selected (villages judged able to proceed in the short term although some items, such as land-lease talks, funding plans and grid connection, still need work). The government offers financing for up to 85% of their solar installation costs. Four Gangwon villages were selected in this first round.
