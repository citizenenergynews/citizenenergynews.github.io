German co-op fully funds 184kWh citizen battery

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.23

Energiegenossenschaft Darmstadt raised the money for a battery using spare grid connection capacity at a Bickenbach business park entirely from citizens on November 20. One share costs 100 euros.

Energiegenossenschaft Darmstadt (EG-DA) in Germany completed financing for its first citizen battery, in Bickenbach, entirely through citizen investment on November 20. The battery has a storage capacity of 184kWh and an output of 92kW, and 79 people subscribed for shares. The cooperative said the battery is ready to start but is still waiting for a registration number.

The fridge-sized battery was designed to use spare capacity (50kW or more) on existing grid connections in places where there is no connection capacity for large batteries. The Bickenbach battery shares the existing connection capacity that companies in the business park do not fully use. The cooperative has also proposed connecting directly to transformers with spare capacity or sharing the grid connections of wind turbines and large solar plants. The battery arrived in August and installation was completed on the 28th. The cooperative explained that now, when electricity sells on the power exchange for 0 cents for several hours a day, is the moment batteries pay off.

The money came from 27 postcodes, led by Bickenbach (64404) with 15.72%, Kleinostheim with 9.98% and Ober-Ramstadt with 9.82%. One cooperative share costs 100 euros, and each member has one vote at the general assembly regardless of how many shares they hold. At its general assembly on September 1, the cooperative approved a dividend for the first time in its 15 years: 4%, or 4 euros per share.

The battery the cooperative already runs is a 17.28kWh plug-in battery at the Agora ABC complex, in operation since April 11. It has applied for a grid connection to place an identical 184kWh battery in Gernsheim, and citizen batteries in Alsbach and at Hofgut Oberfeld are at the stage of having received quotes. The Bickenbach battery is going through the registration process, and its planned start date is around December 1.

Citizen Energy View

Where grid connection capacity is too short for large batteries, 79 citizens used 100-euro shares to put a 184kWh battery on a business park's spare grid connection. In Korea, too, sunlight income villages face saturated distribution lines, and the storage is being handed to private operators. If villages and cooperatives also invest in and own the storage, they can solve the grid problem and keep the income with residents. The government should open the way for village cooperatives to attach small batteries to the spare capacity of existing connections.
