Japan's Agriculture Ministry Revises Agrivoltaics Policy, Adding Under-30% Shading and ¥500,000 Annual Sales Conditions

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.11

At the end of FY2023, 1,221 of 5,167 equipped farm sites (24%) had farming problems; Kyushu's cumulative permits rose 4.4-fold in five years

Japan's Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries revised the basic policy under the Act on Renewable Energy in Rural Areas on October 9, setting new conditions for agrivoltaics (solar panels placed over farmland so farming and power generation go together). The shading rate of the equipment must be under 30%, and the farmer must have at least ¥500,000 a year in production and sales of the crop. The draft revision the ministry presented in explanatory materials in April carried the same two conditions at the same figures.

According to ministry materials, temporary farmland conversion permits for agrivoltaics totaled 6,137 through the end of FY2023, covering 1,361.6 hectares of land beneath the equipment. New permits were 807 in FY2020, 880 in FY2021, 1,020 in FY2022 and 791 in FY2023. Of the 5,167 installations still in place at the end of that year, 1,221 (24%) had problems with farming, up 294 from 927 (22%) the year before (the count excludes installations newly permitted that year). By cause, yield decline or poor growth attributable to the farmer accounted for 872 cases (71%), construction delays 144, decline or poor growth from disasters and similar causes 87, and other reasons 118.

Kyushu's cumulative permits reached 376, up 4.4-fold in five years. A tea farm in Yame, Fukuoka Prefecture, combined the shade structure used to grow gyokuro (a high-grade green tea grown under shade) with power generation equipment, and since March this year has supplied electricity to a local welfare facility, earning ¥70,000–80,000 a month in sales. In a comparison by a University of Tokyo research team, which the ministry used as a reference for the new conditions, rice yields in a paddy with 27% shading averaged 23% lower over six years than in a paddy without equipment, but total revenue including power sales was more than five times higher. Masaya Ishida, research director at the Renewable Energy Institute, said some crops grow well even with shading of 30% or more, so a uniform rule is too strict. On December 23, 2025, a cabinet-level meeting on large-scale solar projects set out measures to clarify desirable agrivoltaics and deal strictly with inappropriate cases.

Citizen Energy View

Beyond shading rate and sales record, the ministry's April draft listed consensus reached in local councils and regional-plan consultations, appropriate benefit sharing from power developers to farmers, and secured removal costs as conditions. The draft also says the guidelines will state that developers and farmers are to negotiate and agree on a benefit-sharing level at or above the amount of lost income.
