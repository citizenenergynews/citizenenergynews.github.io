Tritium at 30,000 becquerels per litre found in Hanul nuclear plant groundwater well; Nuclear Safety and Security Commission opens probe

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.18

Seawater at the intake measured about 117 becquerels; none detected outside the restricted area / KHNP suspects a damaged liquid radioactive waste discharge pipe

South Korea's Nuclear Safety and Security Commission (NSSC) sent an investigation team of Korea Institute of Nuclear Safety (KINS) experts to the Hanul nuclear power plant in Uljin, North Gyeongsang Province, and opened an event investigation on Nov. 16 after tritium was detected in seawater at the plant's intake. The case began when Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power (KHNP) detected tritium at 30,000 becquerels per litre (Bq/L) in one groundwater monitoring well on the plant site on Nov. 13 and reported it to the NSSC's regional office; the figure is close to KHNP's own management limit of 40,000 becquerels.

On Nov. 13 the NSSC had KHNP stop the equipment that could be leaking and asked it to analyse nearby seawater to check whether tritium had escaped. Citing the absence of gamma-emitting nuclides and no anomalies in other monitoring wells or radiation monitors, KHNP believes a pipe that carries filtered liquid radioactive waste out to sea was damaged and part of the discharge leaked. The NSSC plans to use the team to check KHNP's measures and any impact on the surrounding environment.

In seawater results KHNP reported to the NSSC on Nov. 16, the concentration at the intake was about 117 becquerels per litre, and no tritium was detected in seawater sampled outside the plant's restricted area.

Citizen Energy View

Levels in one on-site well reached three quarters of KHNP's own management limit, and the cause is still only KHNP's estimate. Whatever the result outside the restricted area, anxiety will ease only if residents can see measurement data for themselves, published continuously until the suspected pipe damage is confirmed. With the nuclear power public deliberation committee holding its public debate on Nov. 25 on the future power mix, residents' right to safety information near power plants and an expansion of locally owned renewable energy should be put on the table together.
