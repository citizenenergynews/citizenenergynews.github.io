Six Years of Gwangju's Energy Transition Villages: Citizen Solar Reaches 3,975kW

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.26

The Gwangju Climate & Energy Agency presented results on November 24. Twenty-three neighborhoods have taken part, and 115,000 residents joined education and campaigns this year, 2.1 times last year.

The combined capacity of solar power plants run by four solar cooperatives founded by residents of Gwangju's energy transition villages rose to 3,975kW this year, up 772kW from 3,203kW last year. The Gwangju Climate & Energy Agency released the figures at the "2026 Jeonnam-Gwangju Integrated Special City Energy Transition Village Results Sharing Meeting" held on the afternoon of November 24 at the Kimdaejung Convention Center. About 150 residents, activists and representatives of related organizations attended.

The energy transition village program is run by the agency, which supports residents through local hub centers, education and an online platform so they can save energy in their neighborhoods and take part in producing renewable energy. It began with five neighborhoods in 2021, and 23 have taken part through this year; 15 are currently active. This year's neighborhoods are Jiwon and Gyerim in Dong-gu; Pungam and Chipyeong in Seo-gu; Sajik, Bangnim and Bangnim 1 in Nam-gu; Ilgok, Munsan, Samgak, Unam and Dongun in Buk-gu; and Cheomdan, Suwan and Singa in Gwangsan-gu. About 115,000 residents took part in education, promotion and campaigns this year, about 2.1 times the roughly 54,000 last year.

Five citizen solar cooperatives have been set up through village activities, and four of them operate power plants. The agency said these plants generate about 5,224MWh a year and cut greenhouse gas emissions by about 2,262 tonnes. Over six years, a total of about 240,000 residents have taken part, and more than 8,000 education sessions and events have been held.

The agency has presented the program as an urban model (citizen-participation power generation using resident organizations and idle urban spaces), distinct from the government's Sunlight Income Villages, which focus on farming and fishing areas. The government's Sunlight Income Village program selected 528 villages in its first and second rounds, and results of the third round will be announced at the end of December. The agency plans to combine citizen-participation renewable energy projects in idle urban spaces with local benefit-sharing schemes. The program received a minister's award (encouragement prize) at the "2026 Carbon Neutrality Competition" hosted by the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment, and the agency announced the award on November 18.

Citizen Energy View

That four cooperatives of city residents now run nearly 4MW of power plants after six years shows that sunlight income is not just for rural areas. Many worry that cities lack land, but Gwangju's neighborhoods first brought residents together through hub centers and education, and then built plants with their investment. With the launch of the Jeonnam-Gwangju Integrated Special City, a path should be opened to connect this urban experience with rural Sunlight Income Villages. A start can be made by offering apartment rooftops and public building roofs to citizen cooperatives first.
