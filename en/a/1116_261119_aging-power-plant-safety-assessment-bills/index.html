Aging wind turbines and other power plants would face regular safety assessments and removal orders if rated dangerous under two Korean bills open for comment until Nov. 22

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.19

Amendments to the Electrical Safety Management Act and the Electricity Business Act were filed on Nov. 12 / Skipping an assessment would become grounds for license revocation and a fine of up to 3 million won; existing plants would get one year after enforcement for a first assessment

A group of 11 Korean lawmakers on Nov. 12 filed two bills amending the Electrical Safety Management Act and the Electricity Business Act that would require aging power plants to undergo regular safety assessments and allow the government to order them shut down or even removed depending on the results. Both bills were referred to the National Assembly's Climate, Energy, Environment and Labor Committee on Nov. 13, and the Assembly's public notice period runs until Nov. 22. The sponsors said fires and collapses keep occurring at long-running wind turbines and other facilities, yet there is no system to assess aging equipment separately.

The Electrical Safety Management Act amendment would require power generators operating electrical facilities designated by ordinance of the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment to undergo regular safety assessments by the minister. To be assessed, a generator would first need a detailed safety diagnosis from a registered specialist firm that meets capital, technical staff and equipment requirements, and would have to submit the diagnosis report. Based on the assessment, the minister would assign a safety grade to the facility and could order repairs, modifications, or a halt or restriction on its use. Skipping the assessment or violating such an order would be punishable by a fine of up to 3 million won, while anyone who falsifies a diagnosis report, and any generator that knowingly submits one, would face up to one year in prison or a fine of up to 10 million won.

The Electricity Business Act amendment adds new grounds for revoking or suspending a business license: failing to undergo the safety assessment, violating an order without good reason, or a serious industrial accident under the Occupational Safety and Health Act that leads the labor minister to request a business suspension. Under the current Article 12, such grounds allow the license to be revoked or the business suspended for up to six months, and a fine of up to 50 million won can replace a suspension that would cause serious inconvenience to electricity users. Facilities whose licenses are revoked or that receive a danger grade set by presidential decree could be ordered removed and the site restored after review by the Electricity Regulatory Commission (no review for businesses licensed by provincial governors). If an operator does not comply, the authorities would carry out the removal under the Administrative Vicarious Execution Act and charge the cost to the operator. Violating a removal order would carry a fine of up to 3 million won.

Both bills would take effect one year after promulgation, and generators subject to assessment at that time would have to complete their first safety assessment within one year of enforcement. The Electricity Business Act amendment assumes passage of the Electrical Safety Management Act amendment, and comments can be submitted on the National Assembly's legislative notice website until Nov. 22.

Citizen Energy View

Managing aging equipment is a task that small power plants run by village cooperatives and "Sunlight Income Villages" cannot avoid either. Because the scope of covered facilities and the timing and items of diagnoses will be set by ministry ordinance, the standards should be differentiated by size so that small citizen-owned plants are not burdened with excessive costs. Safety is the first concern of residents living next to a plant, and plants co-owned by residents have even less reason to hide inspection results. We hope cooperatives and local residents will submit their own comments during the notice period.
