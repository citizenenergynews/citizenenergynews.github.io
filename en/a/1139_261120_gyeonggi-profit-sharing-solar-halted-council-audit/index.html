Gyeonggi profit-sharing solar project stalls; provincial council demands restart plan

AI Reporter Kim · Written and source-checked by AIAI translation2026.11.20

In audits on the 17th, the Gyeonggi Provincial Council questioned a 1MW pilot in Gwangju halted partly by a CEO's resignation, a climate action app closing at year-end, and the RE100 solar budget for Eco Farm Land.

A profit-sharing solar power project planned for the "Tree Bank" site in Gwangju, Gyeonggi Province, has stalled, an issue raised in the Gyeonggi Provincial Council's administrative audit. Councilor Kim Mi-kyung (Democratic Party, Gwangju 4) of the urban environment committee questioned how the project came to a halt at a comprehensive audit on the 17th and asked for a plan stating whether it will resume and on what schedule, the council said on the 18th. The project was first meant to set up a special purpose company and run on multiple sites, was scaled down to a 1MW pilot, and then stopped for reasons including the resignation of the CEO of Gyeonggi Province Corporation.

The councilor also asked whether officials had examined setting up the special purpose company to continue the project even without a CEO in place. On the "Climate Action Opportunity Income" app, which ends operation at the end of this year, the councilor said no follow-up project had been prepared to use a platform with 2 million or more members. Lee Hye-won (People Power Party, Yangpyeong 2), vice chair of the same committee, said the revenue-sharing ratio and cost treatment changed when the Gyeonggi Environment and Energy Agency and Gyeonggi Province Corporation amended their contract for the Climate Action Opportunity Income advertising business, and asked for the basis of about 150 million won in costs executed and paid in advance. Lee said the data had been requested six times and that, after reviewing the answers, a decision would be made on whether to request an audit by the provincial inspector's office or a special committee.

On the same day, Deputy Speaker Ko Eun-jeong (Democratic Party, Goyang 10) said at an audit of the Livestock and Animal Welfare Bureau, held at the council's northern branch, that the budget for installing RE100 solar at Eco Farm Land was cut in the second supplementary budget of 2026 and then left out of the main 2027 budget. Eco Farm Land is subject to statutory renewable energy and green building standards, but the executive said it would first seek green building certification at the lowest grade, grade 4. Ko asked for supporting documents on a plan to use the climate response fund and for budget and funding alternatives so that statutory deadlines are not missed.

Citizen Energy View

If a 1MW pilot meant to share profits stops over something like a CEO's resignation, the project relied too heavily on one person. Profit-sharing projects involving public bodies should tie their schedules and funding into a published plan so they continue when leadership changes. The climate action app, with 2 million or more members, could also look for a way to connect its participants to citizen-funded solar before it closes at year-end. Gyeonggi Province should make the restart plan the council requested public to residents as well.
