South Gyeongsang research station halts plant factories as power bill jumps 72%

AI Reporter Kim · Checked by the editorAI translation2026.11.20

While the station's industrial (B) tariff rose from 105.5 won to 181.9 won per kWh, its operating budget fell 7.3%. The provincial council raised the issue in a budget review on the 19th.

The Vegetable and Floriculture Research Institute of the South Gyeongsang Agricultural Research and Extension Services has stopped running its two plant factory buildings because it could not cover its electricity bills. Kim Su-bong (Haman 2), a member of the South Gyeongsang Provincial Council's agriculture and maritime affairs committee, raised the issue on the 19th during a review of the province's fourth supplementary budget for 2026 at the council's 438th regular session.

According to data the Agricultural Research and Extension Services submitted to the council, the industrial (B) electricity rate applied to the institute rose 72.4%, from 105.5 won per kWh in 2021 to 181.9 won. Over the same period the institute's public operating budget fell each year, from 578 million won in 2021 to 563 million won in 2022, 553 million won in 2023 and 536 million won in 2024, and it has been held at that level through 2026. That is 42 million won, or about 7.3%, less than in 2021.

From October 24, 2024, the government and Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) froze residential and general-use rates and raised only industrial rates, by an average of 9.7%; the increase for industrial (B), which covers large-volume customers, was 10.2%. KEPCO said at the time that despite six rate increases since 2022, its accumulated deficit from 2021 to the first half of 2024 had reached 41 trillion won (consolidated).

The institute operates a 90-square-metre breeding tissue culture room, 30 experimental greenhouses and two plant factory buildings, and the tissue culture room and plant factories must maintain temperature and equipment all year round. In winter, electric boilers and heating cables are used to warm the greenhouses, pushing power use higher. The province's proposed supplementary budget adds 40,479,000 won to cover the electricity shortfall, which would raise the institute's public utility charges and taxes budget from 89,797,000 won to 130,276,000 won. Kim asked why a problem already raised during a site visit in last year's chrysanthemum growing season had not been reflected in the main budget, when the plant factories had stopped, and whether research schedules had been affected. Kim also checked whether the 2027 main budget includes operating funds based on each facility's actual power demand.

Citizen Energy View

With the electricity rate up 72.4% while the operating budget fell 7.3%, the shutdown of research facilities was foreseeable. Filling the gap with a supplementary budget every time rates rise will only repeat the same problem every winter. Public research facilities that use a lot of power should be the first to look for ways to cut the electricity they buy, through their own solar power and efficiency improvements. The province should publish power use and bills for each Agricultural Research and Extension Services facility and include a plan to cut energy costs in its 2027 budget.
